## This Week's Goals

- Understand approximants as consonants with minimal constriction
- Master lateral approximant symbols (l-sounds)
- Learn the diverse rhotic consonant family (r-sounds)
- Distinguish glides/semivowels from true vowels
- Transcribe liquids and rhotics across languages

## Grammar Focus

### Approximants Overview

**Rule:** Approximants are consonants produced with articulators approaching each other but not creating turbulent airflow (unlike fricatives).

**Types:**
- **Lateral approximants:** Air flows around sides of tongue (**[l]**, **[ɭ]**, **[ʎ]**)
- **Central approximants:** Air flows over center of tongue (**[ɹ]**, **[j]**, **[w]**, **[ɰ]**)
- **Rhotics:** The "r-sound" family (**[r]**, **[ɹ]**, **[ɻ]**, **[ʀ]**, **[ʁ]**)

**Key distinction:** Approximants have less constriction than fricatives (no turbulence) but more than vowels (functioning as consonants).

### Lateral Approximants (L-sounds)

**Rule:** Lateral approximants are produced with tongue blade contacting the roof of the mouth while air flows around one or both sides.

**Complete lateral paradigm:**

| Place | Symbol | Language examples |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| **Alveolar** | **[l]** | English "light" **[laɪt]**, Spanish "lado" **[ˈlaðo]** |
| **Retroflex** | **[ɭ]** | Tamil, Telugu (Indian languages) |
| **Palatal** | **[ʎ]** | Italian "gli" **[ʎi]**, Spanish "llave" **[ˈʎaβe]** |
| **Velar** | **[ʟ]** | Some varieties of English "bottle" (dark l) |

**Light vs. dark L:**

English has two **[l]** variants (allophones):
- **Clear/light [l]:** Occurs before vowels - "light" **[laɪt]**, "fly" **[flaɪ]**
- **Dark/velarized [ɫ]:** Occurs after vowels or word-finally - "bell" **[bɛɫ]**, "bottle" **[ˈbɑtɫ̩]**

Examples:
- "leaf" **[lif]** - clear **[l]**
- "feel" **[fiɫ]** - dark **[ɫ]**
- "million" **[ˈmɪljən]** - clear **[l]** before **[j]**

**Syllabic lateral:**

**[l̩]** or **[l̩]** - Syllabic l (forms syllable nucleus without vowel)

Examples:
- "bottle" **[ˈbɑtl̩]** - syllabic dark **[l]**
- "little" **[ˈlɪtl̩]** - syllabic dark **[l]**
- "pedal" **[ˈpɛdl̩]** - syllabic dark **[l]**

**Other languages:**

- Italian "famiglia" (family) **[faˈmiʎʎa]** - geminate palatal lateral
- Spanish "caballo" (horse) **[kaˈβaʎo]** - palatal lateral (many dialects now use **[j]**)
- Russian "л" **[l]** - always clear/hard
- Russian "ль" **[lʲ]** - palatalized lateral

### Rhotics (R-sounds)

**Rule:** Rhotics are a diverse family of "r-sounds" varying widely across languages. No single articulatory definition unifies them.

**Common rhotic types:**

| Symbol | Type | Language examples |
|--------|------|-------------------|
| **[r]** | Alveolar trill | Spanish "perro" **[ˈpero]**, Italian "r", Russian "р" |
| **[ɾ]** | Alveolar tap/flap | Spanish "pero" **[ˈpeɾo]**, American English "latter" **[ˈlæɾɚ]** |
| **[ɹ]** | Alveolar approximant | English "red" **[ɹɛd]**, "try" **[tɹaɪ]** |
| **[ɻ]** | Retroflex approximant | Mandarin "r" **[ɻ]**, American English "r" (some speakers) |
| **[ʀ]** | Uvular trill | Some German, French regional |
| **[ʁ]** | Uvular fricative | Standard French "r", Standard German "r" |
| **[ɽ]** | Retroflex flap | Hindi, Urdu (contrasts with **[ɾ]**) |

**Trill vs. tap/flap:**

- **Trill [r]:** Multiple rapid vibrations - Spanish "perro" (dog) **[ˈpero]** has 2-3 taps
- **Tap/flap [ɾ]:** Single brief contact - Spanish "pero" (but) **[ˈpeɾo]** has 1 tap

Minimal pair:
- Spanish "caro" (expensive) **[ˈkaɾo]** - single tap
- Spanish "carro" (car) **[ˈkaro]** - trill

**English rhotics:**

Most English has **[ɹ]** (alveolar approximant), NOT a trill:
- "red" **[ɹɛd]**
- "car" **[kʰɑɹ]** (rhotic dialects like American)
- "car" **[kʰɑː]** (non-rhotic dialects like British RP)

American English "r" in "butter" **[ˈbʌɾɚ]**:
- The **[t]** becomes a tap **[ɾ]**
- Final "r" is approximant **[ɹ]** or rhotic vowel **[ɚ]**

**French/German rhotics:**

Modern Standard French and German use uvular **[ʁ]**:
- French "rouge" **[ʁuʒ]** - uvular fricative
- German "rot" (red) **[ʁoːt]** - uvular fricative
- Older/regional varieties may use trilled **[r]** or **[ʀ]**

**Rhotic vowels:**

In some dialects, "r" is vocalized (becomes part of the vowel):
- **[ɚ]** - Rhotic schwa: American "butter" **[ˈbʌɾɚ]**
- **[ɝ]** - Rhotic mid-central: American "bird" **[bɝd]**
- **[ɑɹ]**, **[ɔɹ]**, **[eɹ]** - Vowel + approximant: "car", "core", "care"

### Central Approximants and Glides

**Rule:** Central approximants (glides/semivowels) are consonant-like sounds that quickly glide to an adjacent vowel.

**Common glides:**

| Symbol | Description | Language examples |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **[j]** | Palatal approximant | English "yes" **[jɛs]**, German "ja" **[jaː]** |
| **[w]** | Labiovelar approximant | English "wet" **[wɛt]**, Spanish "huevo" **[ˈweβo]** |
| **[ɥ]** | Labiopalatal approximant | French "huit" (eight) **[ɥit]** |
| **[ɰ]** | Velar approximant | Korean "의" **[ɰi]** |
| **[ʋ]** | Labiodental approximant | Dutch "w", Hindi "व" **[ʋ]** |

**[j] vs. [i], [w] vs. [u]:**

- **[j]** is consonantal (quick glide): "yes" **[jɛs]**
- **[i]** is vocalic (steady vowel): "see" **[si]**
- **[w]** is consonantal: "wet" **[wɛt]**
- **[u]** is vocalic: "boot" **[but]**

**Distinction:** Glides are **non-syllabic** (cannot form syllable nucleus), while vowels are **syllabic**.

Examples:
- "you" **[ju]** - glide **[j]** + vowel **[u]**
- "way" **[weɪ]** - glide **[w]** + diphthong **[eɪ]**
- French "oui" (yes) **[wi]** - glide **[w]** + vowel **[i]**

**Labialization and palatalization:**

Consonants can be modified with **[w]** or **[j]** quality:
- **[◌ʷ]** - Labialized (lip rounding): **[kʷ]**, **[tʷ]**
- **[◌ʲ]** - Palatalized (raised tongue): Russian **[tʲ]**, **[nʲ]**

Examples:
- Russian "тя" (you, accusative) **[tʲa]** - palatalized **[t]**
- Some English "twin" **[tʷɪn]** - labialized **[t]** (anticipatory rounding)

## Vocabulary

### Approximant Types
- **Approximant** - Consonant with minimal constriction, no turbulence
- **Lateral approximant** - Air flows around sides of tongue
- **Central approximant** - Air flows over center of tongue
- **Rhotic** - Member of the "r-sound" family
- **Glide** - Semivowel that quickly transitions to adjacent vowel
- **Liquid** - Collective term for laterals and rhotics

### Phonetic Symbols (Laterals)
- **[l]** - Alveolar lateral approximant
- **[ɭ]** - Retroflex lateral approximant
- **[ʎ]** - Palatal lateral approximant
- **[ɫ]** - Velarized (dark) lateral
- **[ʟ]** - Velar lateral

### Phonetic Symbols (Rhotics)
- **[r]** - Alveolar trill
- **[ɾ]** - Alveolar tap/flap
- **[ɹ]** - Alveolar approximant (English "r")
- **[ɻ]** - Retroflex approximant
- **[ʀ]** - Uvular trill
- **[ʁ]** - Uvular fricative (French/German "r")
- **[ɚ]**, **[ɝ]** - Rhotic vowels

### Phonetic Symbols (Glides)
- **[j]** - Palatal approximant (English "y")
- **[w]** - Labiovelar approximant
- **[ɥ]** - Labiopalatal approximant (French)
- **[ɰ]** - Velar approximant
- **[ʋ]** - Labiodental approximant

### Diacritics
- **[◌̩]** - Syllabic (consonant forms syllable nucleus)
- **[◌ʷ]** - Labialized (with lip rounding)
- **[◌ʲ]** - Palatalized (with tongue raising)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Describing Lateral and Rhotic Articulation

Practice identifying and describing lateral and rhotic consonants.

Key phrases:
- "**[l]** is an alveolar lateral approximant"
- "Air flows around the sides of the tongue for laterals"
- "Spanish has both a trill **[r]** and a tap **[ɾ]**"
- "English uses **[ɹ]**, not a trill"

### Distinguishing Rhotics Across Languages

Practice recognizing different r-sound realizations in various languages.

Key phrases:
- "French uses uvular **[ʁ]**, not alveolar **[r]**"
- "This is a single tap **[ɾ]**, not a trill"
- "American English 'r' is an approximant **[ɹ]**"

## Practice Patterns

1. **Lateral identification:** Recognize **[l]**, **[ɭ]**, **[ʎ]**, **[ɫ]** in transcriptions
2. **Light vs. dark L:** Distinguish "leap" **[lip]** vs. "peel" **[piɫ]**
3. **Rhotic discrimination:** Identify **[r]** vs. **[ɾ]** vs. **[ɹ]** vs. **[ʁ]**
4. **Trill vs. tap:** Spanish "perro" **[ˈpero]** vs. "pero" **[ˈpeɾo]**
5. **Syllabic consonants:** Transcribe "bottle" **[ˈbɑtl̩]**, "button" **[ˈbʌtn̩]**
6. **Glide transcription:** Transcribe "yes" **[jɛs]**, "wet" **[wɛt]**, French "oui" **[wi]**
7. **Rhotic vowels:** American "bird" **[bɝd]**, "butter" **[ˈbʌɾɚ]**

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Writing "r" for English → English uses **[ɹ]** (approximant), not **[r]** (trill)
- **[r] vs. [ɾ]** → Spanish "perro" has trill **[r]**, "pero" has tap **[ɾ]** (different sounds)
- **[l] vs. [ɫ]** → "light" has clear **[l]**, "pill" has dark **[ɫ]** (positional variants)
- Using **[j]** for "judge" → **[j]** is "y" sound in "yes", not "j" sound in "judge" **[dʒ]**
- **[ʎ] vs. [lj]** → Italian "gli" is single palatal **[ʎ]**, not sequence **[lj]**
- Confusing **[w]** and **[v]** → **[w]** is approximant (no turbulence), **[v]** is fricative
- Missing syllabic marker → "bottle" is **[ˈbɑtl̩]** not **[ˈbɑtəl]**
- **[ʁ] vs. [r]** → French "rouge" uses uvular **[ʁ]**, not alveolar trill **[r]**
- **[ɹ] vs. [ɻ]** → **[ɹ]** is alveolar (tongue tip up), **[ɻ]** is retroflex (tongue tip curled back)
- Not recognizing **[ɚ]/[ɝ]** → American "father" **[ˈfɑðɚ]** has rhotic schwa, not **[fɑðər]**

## Cultural Notes

- The English "r" sound **[ɹ]** is relatively rare globally; most languages use trills **[r]** or taps **[ɾ]**
- Spanish distinguishes trill **[r]** and tap **[ɾ]** as separate phonemes (minimal pairs like "caro"/"carro")
- French and German shifted from alveolar **[r]** to uvular **[ʁ]** in the 17th-19th centuries
- Non-rhotic English dialects (British RP, Australian) drop **[ɹ]** after vowels: "car" **[kʰɑː]** not **[kʰɑɹ]**
- The palatal lateral **[ʎ]** is disappearing in modern Spanish, often replaced by **[j]**
- Japanese has a rhotic that varies between **[ɾ]**, **[ɺ]** (lateral flap), and **[ɹ]** depending on context
- Russian has a "hard L" **[l]** and "soft L" **[lʲ]** (palatalized) as distinct phonemes
- Dark **[ɫ]** in English is actually velarized (tongue back raised toward velum) while tongue tip contacts alveolar ridge
