## This Week's Goals

- Understand consonant description using place, manner, and voicing
- Master the complete plosive (stop) consonant paradigm
- Learn all nasal consonant symbols
- Recognize voicing contrasts in minimal pairs
- Transcribe plosives and nasals accurately in multiple languages

## Grammar Focus

### Consonant Description Framework

**Rule:** Every consonant is described using three features: voicing + place of articulation + manner of articulation.

**Format:** [voicing] + [place] + [manner]

Examples:
- **[p]** = voiceless + bilabial + plosive
- **[n]** = voiced + alveolar + nasal
- **[k]** = voiceless + velar + plosive

**Voicing:**
- **Voiceless:** Vocal cords do not vibrate (**[p]**, **[t]**, **[k]**, **[f]**, **[s]**)
- **Voiced:** Vocal cords vibrate (**[b]**, **[d]**, **[ɡ]**, **[v]**, **[z]**)

**Place of articulation** (where obstruction occurs):
- **Bilabial:** Two lips (**[p]**, **[b]**, **[m]**)
- **Labiodental:** Lower lip + upper teeth (**[f]**, **[v]**)
- **Dental:** Tongue tip + upper teeth (**[θ]**, **[ð]**)
- **Alveolar:** Tongue tip/blade + alveolar ridge (**[t]**, **[d]**, **[n]**, **[s]**, **[z]**)
- **Postalveolar:** Tongue blade + area behind alveolar ridge (**[ʃ]**, **[ʒ]**)
- **Palatal:** Tongue body + hard palate (**[c]**, **[ɟ]**, **[ɲ]**)
- **Velar:** Tongue back + soft palate (velum) (**[k]**, **[ɡ]**, **[ŋ]**)
- **Uvular:** Tongue back + uvula (**[q]**, **[ɢ]**, **[ɴ]**)
- **Glottal:** At the glottis (vocal cords) (**[ʔ]**, **[h]**)

**Manner of articulation** (how airflow is obstructed):
- **Plosive (stop):** Complete closure then release (**[p]**, **[t]**, **[k]**)
- **Nasal:** Oral closure, air exits through nose (**[m]**, **[n]**, **[ŋ]**)
- **Fricative:** Narrow constriction causing turbulent airflow (**[f]**, **[s]**, **[ʃ]**)
- **Approximant:** Open approximation, no turbulence (**[w]**, **[j]**, **[ɹ]**)

### Plosive Consonants (Complete Paradigm)

**Rule:** Plosives (stops) involve complete closure of the oral cavity followed by a sudden release of airflow.

**Plosive consonant chart (by place and voicing):**

| Place | Voiceless | Voiced | Examples |
|-------|-----------|--------|----------|
| **Bilabial** | **[p]** | **[b]** | "pat" **[pʰæt]**, "bat" **[bæt]** |
| **Alveolar** | **[t]** | **[d]** | "ten" **[tʰɛn]**, "den" **[dɛn]** |
| **Velar** | **[k]** | **[ɡ]** | "cat" **[kʰæt]**, "gap" **[ɡæp]** |
| **Uvular** | **[q]** | **[ɢ]** | Arabic "قلب" (heart) **[qalb]** |
| **Glottal** | **[ʔ]** | — | "uh-oh" **[ˈʔʌʔoʊ]**, Hawaiian "Hawai'i" **[həˈvɐiʔi]** |
| **Palatal** | **[c]** | **[ɟ]** | Hungarian "tyúk" (hen) **[cuːk]** |
| **Retroflex** | **[ʈ]** | **[ɖ]** | Hindi "टमाटर" (tomato) **[ʈəˈmaːʈər]** |

**Aspiration:**

- **[◌ʰ]** - Aspirated (strong breath release): English "pat" **[pʰæt]**, "top" **[tʰɑp]**
- Unaspirated (minimal breath): Spanish "para" **[ˈpaɾa]** (no aspiration)

English voiceless plosives are aspirated word-initially:
- "pin" **[pʰɪn]** - aspirated
- "spin" **[spɪn]** - unaspirated (after **[s]**)
- "nap" **[næp]** - lightly aspirated or unaspirated word-finally

Examples across languages:
- **English:** "top" **[tʰɑp]** - aspirated alveolar plosive
- **Spanish:** "tapa" **[ˈtapa]** - unaspirated alveolar plosive
- **French:** "papa" **[paˈpa]** - unaspirated bilabial plosive
- **German:** "Katze" (cat) **[ˈkʰatsə]** - aspirated velar plosive
- **Arabic:** "قلب" (heart) **[qalb]** - voiceless uvular plosive
- **Hawaiian:** "Hawai'i" **[həˈvɐiʔi]** - glottal stop

**Voicing minimal pairs:**

- English: "pat" **[pʰæt]** vs. "bat" **[bæt]**
- English: "ten" **[tʰɛn]** vs. "den" **[dɛn]**
- English: "cap" **[kʰæp]** vs. "gap" **[ɡæp]**
- French: "pont" (bridge) **[pɔ̃]** vs. "bon" (good) **[bɔ̃]**
- Spanish: "tapa" **[ˈtapa]** vs. "da" (gives) **[ˈda]**

### Nasal Consonants (Complete Paradigm)

**Rule:** Nasals are produced with complete oral closure while air escapes through the nasal cavity. All nasals are voiced.

**Nasal consonant chart (by place):**

| Place | Symbol | Examples |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **Bilabial** | **[m]** | "man" **[mæn]**, "mano" (Spanish: hand) **[ˈmano]** |
| **Labiodental** | **[ɱ]** | "symphony" **[ˈsɪɱfəni]**, "comfort" **[ˈkʰʌɱfət]** |
| **Alveolar** | **[n]** | "no" **[noʊ]**, "nada" (Spanish: nothing) **[ˈnaða]** |
| **Retroflex** | **[ɳ]** | Swedish "barn" (child) **[bɑːɳ]** |
| **Palatal** | **[ɲ]** | Spanish "año" (year) **[ˈaɲo]**, French "agneau" **[aˈɲo]** |
| **Velar** | **[ŋ]** | English "sing" **[sɪŋ]**, "finger" **[ˈfɪŋɡɚ]** |
| **Uvular** | **[ɴ]** | Japanese "さん" (suffix) **[saɴ]** (in some dialects) |

**Important note:** **[ŋ]** is never spelled "ng" in IPA - it is a single sound represented by a single symbol.

Examples:
- English "song" **[sɔŋ]** - velar nasal (one sound, not **[n]** + **[ɡ]**)
- English "finger" **[ˈfɪŋɡɚ]** - **[ŋ]** followed by **[ɡ]** (two sounds)
- Spanish "mango" **[ˈmaŋɡo]** - velar nasal before velar plosive

**Syllabic nasals:**

**[◌̩]** - Syllabic marker (diacritic under consonant): nasal forms syllable nucleus without a vowel

Examples:
- English "button" **[ˈbʌtn̩]** - syllabic **[n]**
- English "prism" **[ˈpɹɪzm̩]** - syllabic **[m]**
- English "bacon" **[ˈbeɪkn̩]** - syllabic **[n]**

**Nasal assimilation:**

Nasals often assimilate to the place of articulation of following consonants:

- "input" **[ˈɪmpʊt]** - **[n]** → **[m]** before bilabial **[p]**
- "incomplete" **[ˌɪŋkəmˈplit]** - **[n]** → **[ŋ]** before velar **[k]**
- "symphony" **[ˈsɪɱfəni]** - **[n]** → **[ɱ]** before labiodental **[f]**

Examples in sentences:
- English: "The man can sing" **[ðə mæn kʰən sɪŋ]**
- Spanish: "Mañana" (tomorrow) **[maˈɲana]**
- French: "Un bon agneau" (a good lamb) **[œ̃ bɔ̃ aˈɲo]**

### Unreleased Plosives

**[◌̚]** - Unreleased (no audible release): plosive closure formed but not released

Common in English final position:
- "stop" **[stɑp̚]** - lips close but don't release
- "cat" **[kʰæt̚]** - tongue remains at alveolar ridge
- "back" **[bæk̚]** - tongue remains at velum

Examples:
- "apt" **[æp̚t]** - unreleased **[p]** before **[t]**
- "act" **[æk̚t]** - unreleased **[k]** before **[t]**

## Vocabulary

### Place of Articulation
- **Bilabial** - Two lips touch (**[p]**, **[b]**, **[m]**)
- **Labiodental** - Lower lip touches upper teeth (**[f]**, **[v]**, **[ɱ]**)
- **Dental** - Tongue touches upper teeth (**[θ]**, **[ð]**)
- **Alveolar** - Tongue touches alveolar ridge (**[t]**, **[d]**, **[n]**, **[s]**)
- **Retroflex** - Tongue tip curls back (**[ʈ]**, **[ɖ]**, **[ɳ]**)
- **Palatal** - Tongue body touches hard palate (**[c]**, **[ɟ]**, **[ɲ]**)
- **Velar** - Tongue back touches soft palate (**[k]**, **[ɡ]**, **[ŋ]**)
- **Uvular** - Tongue back touches uvula (**[q]**, **[ɢ]**, **[ɴ]**)
- **Glottal** - At the vocal cords (**[ʔ]**, **[h]**)

### Manner of Articulation
- **Plosive** - Complete closure then release (also called "stop")
- **Nasal** - Oral closure, nasal airflow
- **Aspiration** - Breath release after plosive
- **Syllabic** - Consonant acts as syllable nucleus

### Phonetic Symbols (Plosives)
- **[p]**, **[b]** - Bilabial plosives
- **[t]**, **[d]** - Alveolar plosives
- **[k]**, **[ɡ]** - Velar plosives (note: IPA uses **[ɡ]** not "g")
- **[q]**, **[ɢ]** - Uvular plosives
- **[ʔ]** - Glottal stop
- **[c]**, **[ɟ]** - Palatal plosives
- **[ʈ]**, **[ɖ]** - Retroflex plosives

### Phonetic Symbols (Nasals)
- **[m]** - Bilabial nasal
- **[ɱ]** - Labiodental nasal
- **[n]** - Alveolar nasal
- **[ɳ]** - Retroflex nasal
- **[ɲ]** - Palatal nasal
- **[ŋ]** - Velar nasal
- **[ɴ]** - Uvular nasal

### Diacritics
- **[◌ʰ]** - Aspirated
- **[◌̚]** - Unreleased
- **[◌̩]** - Syllabic

## Conversation Scenarios

### Describing Consonant Articulation

Practice describing plosives and nasals using the three-feature framework (voicing + place + manner).

Key phrases:
- "**[p]** is a voiceless bilabial plosive"
- "**[ŋ]** is a voiced velar nasal"
- "The tongue back touches the velum for **[k]**"
- "This plosive is aspirated word-initially"

### Identifying Voicing Contrasts

Practice distinguishing voiced and voiceless plosive pairs in minimal pairs and transcriptions.

Key phrases:
- "**[p]** and **[b]** differ only in voicing"
- "English aspirates voiceless plosives at the start of words"
- "The nasal assimilates to the following consonant's place"

## Practice Patterns

1. **Three-feature description:** Describe each plosive and nasal using voicing, place, and manner
2. **Voicing discrimination:** Identify voicing in pairs: **[p]/[b]**, **[t]/[d]**, **[k]/[ɡ]**
3. **Nasal place identification:** Match nasals to their place: **[m]** = bilabial, **[ŋ]** = velar
4. **Aspiration transcription:** Mark aspiration in English plosives: "top" → **[tʰɑp]**
5. **Nasal assimilation:** Transcribe words with assimilated nasals: "input" → **[ˈɪmpʊt]**
6. **Syllabic nasals:** Identify syllabic nasals: "button" → **[ˈbʌtn̩]**
7. **Glottal stop recognition:** Find **[ʔ]** in transcriptions of casual English or Hawaiian
8. **Unreleased plosives:** Mark unreleased plosives in final position: "cat" → **[kʰæt̚]**

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Writing "ng" for **[ŋ]** → Use single symbol **[ŋ]**, not two letters
- Confusing **[ɡ]** with "g" → IPA uses **[ɡ]** (single-story), not "g" (double-story)
- Forgetting aspiration → English "top" is **[tʰɑp]** not **[tɑp]**
- Using **[n]** before velars → "sing" is **[sɪŋ]** not **[sɪn]**
- **[ɱ]** vs. **[m]** → "symphony" has labiodental **[ɱ]** before **[f]**, not bilabial **[m]**
- Not marking syllabic nasals → "button" is **[ˈbʌtn̩]** not **[ˈbʌtən]**
- Confusing **[ɲ]** with "ny" → Spanish "año" is **[ˈaɲo]** (single palatal nasal)
- Missing unreleased markers → "apt" is **[æp̚t]** not **[æpt]** (no release of **[p]**)
- Confusing **[q]** and **[k]** → **[q]** is uvular (further back), **[k]** is velar
- Using **[g]** instead of **[ɡ]** → IPA standard is **[ɡ]** (opentail g), not standard "g"

## Cultural Notes

- The glottal stop **[ʔ]** is a phoneme in Arabic, Hawaiian, and many other languages, but only marginally used in English
- Aspiration of voiceless plosives varies: strong in English, moderate in German, absent in Spanish and French
- The velar nasal **[ŋ]** is a single phoneme in English (as in "sing") but a sequence in Spanish ("mango" = **[n]** + **[ɡ]**)
- Many languages lack **[p]** (Arabic classical, Hawaiian), making it relatively rare globally despite being common in European languages
- Retroflex plosives **[ʈ]**, **[ɖ]** are crucial in Indic languages (Hindi, Sanskrit) but absent in European languages
- Japanese and Korean have lenis (plain) vs. fortis (tense) plosives rather than voicing contrasts
- The palatal nasal **[ɲ]** sounds exotic to English speakers but is common in Spanish, French, Italian, and many other languages
