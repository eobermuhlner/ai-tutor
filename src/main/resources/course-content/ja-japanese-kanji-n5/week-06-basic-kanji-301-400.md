## This Week's Goals

- Learn kanji related to health and medicine
- Master vocabulary for body and health
- Understand emotions and mental state terms
- Practice activity and hobby vocabulary

## Grammar Focus

**Minimal grammar**: Focus on recognizing and reading kanji rather than complex grammatical structures.

**Basic expressions**:
- **Health**: **元気 (げんき)**, **病気 (びょうき)**, **医者 (いしゃ)**
- **Activities**: **趣味 (しゅみ)**, **運動 (うんどう)**, **読書 (どくしょ)**

Examples:
- **元気です** - Genki desu (I am healthy/fine)
- **趣味は読書です** - Shumi wa dokusho desu (My hobby is reading)

## Conversation Scenarios

Practice using kanji related to health, emotions, and activities. The tutor will help you use these characters in context.

Topics to cover:
- Discussing health and visiting doctors
- Expressing emotions and mental states
- Talking about hobbies and activities
- Describing physical conditions

Tutor will prompt: "Can you describe how you're feeling?" or "What are your favorite activities?"

## Practice Patterns

1. Write each kanji 3 times with correct stroke order
2. Create sentences about health and well-being
3. Practice expressing emotions and mental states
4. Use activity and hobby vocabulary in context
5. Describe physical conditions and body parts
6. Practice reading simple phrases with the new characters

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- **Confusing similar kanji** → 心 (heart) vs 思 (think) - different meanings
- **Using wrong readings** → 病院 (びょういん) vs 医者 (いしゃ) - different readings
- **Forgetting context for readings** → Some kanji change meaning in compounds
- **Mixing up similar concepts** → 静 (quiet) vs 動 (move) - opposite meanings
- **Stroke order errors** → Health kanji often have complex strokes

## Cultural Notes

- Health check-ups (人間ドック) are common in Japan
- The concept of "forest bathing" (shinrin-yoku) is part of Japanese wellness culture
- Many traditional Japanese activities like tea ceremony and calligraphy emphasize mindfulness
- Mental health awareness is growing in Japan
- Community sports and group activities are popular
- Traditional Japanese medicine (Kampo) is still practiced alongside Western medicine
