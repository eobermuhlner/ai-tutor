## This Week's Goals

- Learn kanji related to government and society
- Master vocabulary for politics and administration
- Understand law and justice terms
- Practice culture and arts vocabulary

## Grammar Focus

**Minimal grammar**: Focus on recognizing and reading kanji rather than complex grammatical structures.

**Basic expressions**:
- **Government**: **政府 (せいふ)**, **法律 (ほうりつ)**, **国会 (こっかい)**
- **Culture**: **文化 (ぶんか)**, **芸術 (げいじゅつ)**, **伝統 (でんとう)**

Examples:
- **法律を守ります** - Houritsu wo mamorimasu (I follow the law)
- **文化を学びます** - Bunka wo manabimasu (I study culture)

## Conversation Scenarios

Practice using kanji related to government, law, and culture. The tutor will help you use these characters in context.

Topics to cover:
- Discussing government and civic responsibilities
- Talking about laws and rights
- Expressing opinions about politics
- Describing cultural experiences

Tutor will prompt: "Can you explain what a law is?" or "What cultural activities interest you?"

## Practice Patterns

1. Write each kanji 3 times with correct stroke order
2. Create sentences about government and society
3. Practice expressing civic concepts
4. Use law and justice vocabulary in context
5. Describe cultural activities and arts
6. Practice reading simple phrases with the new characters

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- **Confusing similar kanji** → 政 (politics) vs 法 (law) - different meanings
- **Using wrong readings** → 国会 (こっかい) vs 市民 (しみん) - different readings
- **Forgetting context for readings** → Some kanji change meaning in compounds
- **Mixing up similar concepts** → 正 (correct) vs 義 (justice) - different aspects
- **Stroke order errors** → Government kanji often have complex strokes

## Cultural Notes

- Japan has a parliamentary system with Diet as the legislative body
- The concept of "wa" (harmony) is important in Japanese society
- Many traditional Japanese arts like tea ceremony and flower arrangement are still practiced
- Japanese law combines elements of German, French, and American legal systems
- Cultural preservation is highly valued in Japan
- Civic responsibility and social harmony are emphasized in Japanese culture
