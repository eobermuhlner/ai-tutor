## This Week's Goals

- Understand how sounds change in connected speech
- Read IPA transcriptions showing linking and liaison
- Recognize assimilation patterns in transcriptions
- Identify elided (deleted) sounds in rapid speech
- Practice reading natural, fluent speech transcriptions

## Grammar Focus

### Linking and Liaison

**Rule:** In fluent speech, words connect together. Consonants link to following vowels across word boundaries.

**English linking:**

**Consonant-to-vowel linking:**
- "pick it up" **[ˈpɪk ɪt ʌp]** → **[ˈpɪkɪɾʌp]** - sounds like one word
- "tell us" **[tɛl ʌs]** → **[ˈtɛləs]**
- "an apple" **[æn ˈæpəl]** → **[ənˈæpəl]**

**Intrusive /r/ (non-rhotic English):**
- "law and order" **[lɔː ən ˈɔːdə]** → **[lɔːɹ ən ˈɔːdə]** - extra **[ɹ]** inserted!
- "idea of" **[aɪˈdɪə ɒv]** → **[aɪˈdɪəɹ ɒv]**

**Intrusive /w/ and /j/:**
- "go away" **[ɡəʊ əˈweɪ]** → **[ɡəʊwəˈweɪ]** - **[w]** glide inserted
- "see it" **[siː ɪt]** → **[siːjɪt]** - **[j]** glide inserted

**French liaison:**

**Required liaison** (linking silent consonant):
- "les amis" (the friends) **[le.z‿a.mi]** - silent "s" links as **[z]**
- "un homme" (a man) **[œ̃.n‿ɔm]** - silent "n" links
- "vous avez" (you have) **[vu.z‿a.ve]** - silent "s" links as **[z]**

**Forbidden liaison:**
- "et il" (and he) **[e il]** - NO liaison, not **[e.t‿il]**

**Optional liaison** (varies by register):
- "nous avons" (we have) **[nu a.vɔ̃]** or **[nu.z‿a.vɔ̃]**

### Assimilation

**Rule:** Sounds change to become more like neighboring sounds. Common in all languages.

**Place assimilation (English):**

**Nasal place assimilation:**
- "input" **[ˈɪnpʊt]** → **[ˈɪmpʊt]** - **[n]** → **[m]** before **[p]**
- "incomplete" **[ɪnkəmˈplit]** → **[ɪŋkəmˈplit]** - **[n]** → **[ŋ]** before **[k]**
- "comfort" **[ˈkʌnfɚt]** → **[ˈkʌɱfɚt]** - **[n]** → **[ɱ]** before **[f]**

**Alveolar assimilation:**
- "that person" **[ðæt ˈpɝsən]** → **[ðæp ˈpɝsən]** - **[t]** → **[p]** before **[p]**
- "good girl" **[ɡʊd ɡɝl]** → **[ɡʊɡ ɡɝl]** - **[d]** → **[ɡ]** before **[ɡ]**

**Voicing assimilation:**

**English plural/past:**
- "cats" **[kæts]** - voiceless **[s]** after voiceless **[t]**
- "dogs" **[dɔɡz]** - voiced **[z]** after voiced **[ɡ]**
- "walked" **[wɔkt]** - voiceless **[t]** after voiceless **[k]**
- "begged" **[bɛɡd]** - voiced **[d]** after voiced **[ɡ]**

**Russian final devoicing:**
- "год" (year) **[ɡot]** - underlying **/ɡod/**, pronounced **[t]** (devoiced)
- "хлеб" (bread) **[xlʲep]** - underlying **/xlʲeb/**, pronounced **[p]**

**Palatalization assimilation:**

**English yod-coalescence:**
- "would you" **[wʊd ju]** → **[ˈwʊdʒu]** or **[ˈwʊdʒə]** - **[d]** + **[j]** → **[dʒ]**
- "got you" **[ɡɑt ju]** → **[ˈɡɑtʃu]** - **[t]** + **[j]** → **[tʃ]**
- "miss you" **[mɪs ju]** → **[ˈmɪʃu]** - **[s]** + **[j]** → **[ʃ]**

### Elision (Deletion)

**Rule:** Sounds are deleted in rapid or casual speech, especially unstressed syllables.

**English elision:**

**Weak vowel deletion:**
- "police" **[pəˈlis]** → **[plis]** - schwa deleted
- "today" **[təˈdeɪ]** → **[tˈdeɪ]** or **[tdeɪ]** - schwa deleted
- "suppose" **[səˈpoʊz]** → **[spoʊz]** - schwa deleted

**Consonant deletion:**
- "next week" **[nɛkst wik]** → **[nɛks wik]** - **[t]** deleted before **[w]**
- "handsome" **[hændsəm]** → **[hænsəm]** - **[d]** deleted
- "postman" **[poʊstmæn]** → **[poʊsmæn]** - **[t]** deleted

**H-dropping (some dialects):**
- "give him" **[ɡɪv hɪm]** → **[ˈɡɪvɪm]** - **[h]** deleted
- "tell her" **[tɛl hɝ]** → **[ˈtɛlɚ]** - **[h]** deleted

**French e-muet deletion:**
- "je ne sais pas" (I don't know) **[ʒə nə sɛ pa]** → **[ʃnɛ pa]** or **[ʃsɛ pa]**
- "petit" (small) **[pəˈti]** → **[pˈti]**

### Flapping/Tapping

**Rule:** In American English, /t/ and /d/ become a tap **[ɾ]** between vowels.

**American English flapping:**
- "water" **[ˈwɔtɚ]** → **[ˈwɔɾɚ]** - **[t]** → **[ɾ]**
- "ladder" **[ˈlædɚ]** → **[ˈlæɾɚ]** - **[d]** → **[ɾ]**
- "butter" **[ˈbʌtɚ]** → **[ˈbʌɾɚ]** - **[t]** → **[ɾ]**
- "better" **[ˈbɛtɚ]** → **[ˈbɛɾɚ]** - **[t]** → **[ɾ]**

Result: "latter" **[ˈlæɾɚ]** and "ladder" **[ˈlæɾɚ]** sound identical!

**Spanish tapping:**
- "pero" (but) **[ˈpeɾo]** - single tap always
- "para" (for) **[ˈpaɾa]** - single tap always

### Stress and Rhythm

**Rule:** Stress creates rhythm patterns. English is stress-timed, Romance languages are syllable-timed.

**English stress-timing:**

Strong-weak patterns:
- "BAnana" **[bəˈnænə]** - stress on **NAN**
- "phoTOgraphy" **[fəˈtɑɡɹəfi]** - stress on **TOG**
- "underSTAND" **[ˌʌndɚˈstænd]** - primary stress on **STAND**, secondary on **UN**

Weak forms (function words):
- "and" - strong **[ænd]**, weak **[ənd]** or **[ən]** or **[n̩]**
- "can" - strong **[kæn]**, weak **[kən]** or **[kn̩]**
- "to" - strong **[tu]**, weak **[tə]** or **[tʊ]**
- "the" - strong **[ði]** (before vowels), weak **[ðə]**

**Spanish syllable-timing:**
- Each syllable roughly equal duration
- "ma-ña-na" **[ma.ˈɲa.na]** - equal syllable weight
- Less vowel reduction than English

**French syllable-timing:**
- Even syllable rhythm with final stress
- "En-chan-té" **[ɑ̃.ʃɑ̃.ˈte]** - stress on final syllable

## Conversation Scenarios

### Reading Linked Speech

Practice reading transcriptions showing linking, liaison, and connected speech features.

Key phrases to practice:
- "Pick it up" **[ˈpɪkɪɾʌp]** - sounds like one word
- French "les amis" **[le.z‿a.mi]** - liaison with **[z]**
- "I'll call you later" **[aɪl kɔl ju ˈleɪɾɚ]** - multiple connections

### Recognizing Assimilation

Practice reading transcriptions with assimilated sounds.

Key phrases to practice:
- "input" **[ˈɪmpʊt]** - **[n]** becomes **[m]**
- "would you" **[ˈwʊdʒu]** - **[d]** + **[j]** becomes **[dʒ]**
- "good boy" **[ɡʊb bɔɪ]** - **[d]** becomes **[b]**

### Reading Rapid Speech

Practice reading casual, rapid speech transcriptions with elision and reduction.

Key phrases to practice:
- "going to" **[ˈɡʌnə]** (not **[ˈɡoʊɪŋ tu]**)
- "want to" **[ˈwɑnə]** (not **[wɑnt tu]**)
- "probably" **[ˈpɹɑbli]** (not **[ˈpɹɑbəbli]**)

## Practice Patterns

1. **Linking practice:** Read "pick_it_up", "turn_it_off" as connected
2. **Flapping recognition:** Identify **[ɾ]** in "water", "better", "city"
3. **Assimilation identification:** Spot place changes in "input", "handbag"
4. **Elision detection:** Find deleted sounds in "probably", "comfortable"
5. **Weak form reading:** Read "the", "and", "to" in unstressed positions
6. **French liaison:** Read "les_amis", "vous_avez" with linking
7. **Rhythm patterns:** Mark primary **[ˈ]** and secondary **[ˌ]** stress

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Reading word-by-word → "pick it up" sounds like **[ˈpɪkɪɾʌp]**, not **[pɪk ɪt ʌp]** in fluent speech
- Missing flap → American "water" is **[ˈwɔɾɚ]**, not **[ˈwɔtɚ]** in natural speech
- Not recognizing assimilation → "input" is **[ˈɪmpʊt]**, not **[ˈɪnpʊt]**
- Missing elision → "probably" is often **[ˈpɹɑbli]**, not **[ˈpɹɑbəbli]**
- Weak forms as strong → "and" in connected speech is **[ən]**, not **[ænd]**
- French liaison errors → "les amis" is **[le.z‿a.mi]**, not **[le a.mi]** (must link!)
- **[ɾ] vs. [r]** → American flap **[ɾ]** is different from Spanish trill **[r]**
- Missing yod-coalescence → "would you" is **[ˈwʊdʒu]**, not **[wʊd ju]** in casual speech
- Syllable-timed reading of English → English reduces unstressed syllables significantly
- Over-pronouncing function words → "the" is usually **[ðə]**, not **[ði]**

## Cultural Notes

- Connected speech features are normal in ALL languages - transcribing word-by-word doesn't reflect natural pronunciation
- English stress-timing creates strong weak-forms and extensive vowel reduction
- French liaison is rule-governed: some contexts require it, others forbid it, others are optional
- American flapping makes "latter" and "ladder" homophones (same pronunciation)
- Russian final devoicing means spelling doesn't match pronunciation (orthography vs. phonetics)
- British and American English differ significantly in connected speech patterns
- Formal vs. casual speech affects how much elision and assimilation occur
- Learning connected speech features helps learners sound more natural and understand fast speech
