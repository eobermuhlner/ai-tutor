## Grammar Focus

### Spanish Vowels and Consonants

**Rule:** Spanish has 5 pure vowel sounds (no diphthongs in single vowels) and simpler consonant system than English.

**Spanish vowels:**
- **[a]** - "casa" (house) **[ˈkasa]**, "mañana" **[maˈɲana]**
- **[e]** - "peso" (weight) **[ˈpeso]**, "leche" (milk) **[ˈletʃe]**
- **[i]** - "sí" (yes) **[si]**, "niño" (boy) **[ˈniɲo]**
- **[o]** - "como" (like) **[ˈkomo]**, "loco" (crazy) **[ˈloko]**
- **[u]** - "tú" (you) **[tu]**, "azul" (blue) **[aˈsul]**

No English-style **[ɪ]**, **[ʊ]**, **[ʌ]**, or **[ə]** - Spanish vowels are always "pure"!

**Spanish consonants different from English:**
- **[ɾ]** - Single tap "r": "pero" (but) **[ˈpeɾo]**, "cara" (face) **[ˈkaɾa]**
- **[r]** - Trilled "rr": "perro" (dog) **[ˈpero]**, "carro" (car) **[ˈkaro]**
- **[ɲ]** - Palatal nasal "ñ": "año" (year) **[ˈaɲo]**, "España" **[esˈpaɲa]**
- **[x]** - Velar fricative "j": "jamón" (ham) **[xaˈmon]**, "rojo" (red) **[ˈroxo]**
- **[β]**, **[ð]**, **[ɣ]** - Soft variants of b, d, g between vowels

Examples:
- "gracias" (thank you) **[ˈɡɾasjas]** or **[ˈɡɾaθjas]** (Spain)
- "buenos días" (good morning) **[ˈbwenos ˈdias]**
- "hola" (hello) **[ˈola]** - no "h" sound!
- "por favor" (please) **[poɾ faˈβoɾ]**

### French Nasal Vowels and R

**Rule:** French has 4 nasal vowels (air flows through nose) and uses uvular **[ʁ]** instead of English **[ɹ]**.

**French nasal vowels:**
- **[ɛ̃]** - "vin" (wine) **[vɛ̃]**, "pain" (bread) **[pɛ̃]**
- **[ɑ̃]** - "sans" (without) **[sɑ̃]**, "temps" (time) **[tɑ̃]**
- **[ɔ̃]** - "bon" (good) **[bɔ̃]**, "nom" (name) **[nɔ̃]**
- **[œ̃]** - "un" (one) **[œ̃]** - disappearing in modern French

**French rounded front vowels:**
- **[y]** - "tu" (you) **[ty]**, "rue" (street) **[ʁy]**
- **[ø]** - "peu" (few) **[pø]**, "deux" (two) **[dø]**
- **[œ]** - "peur" (fear) **[pœʁ]**, "sœur" (sister) **[sœʁ]**

**French uvular r:**
- **[ʁ]** - "rouge" (red) **[ʁuʒ]**, "Paris" **[paˈʁi]**, "frère" (brother) **[fʁɛʁ]**

Examples:
- "bonjour" (hello) **[bɔ̃ˈʒuʁ]**
- "merci" (thank you) **[mɛʁˈsi]**
- "je t'aime" (I love you) **[ʒə tɛm]**
- "s'il vous plaît" (please) **[sil vu plɛ]**
- "au revoir" (goodbye) **[o ʁəˈvwaʁ]**

### German Umlauts and Consonants

**Rule:** German has rounded front vowels (ü, ö) and two types of "ch" sounds based on vowel context.

**German rounded front vowels (umlauts):**
- **[y]** or **[yː]** - "über" (over) **[ˈyːbɐ]**, "Glück" (luck) **[ɡlʏk]**
- **[ʏ]** - short version: "hübsch" (pretty) **[hʏpʃ]**
- **[ø]** or **[øː]** - "schön" (beautiful) **[ʃøːn]**, "Öl" (oil) **[øːl]**
- **[œ]** - short version: "können" (can) **[ˈkœnən]**

**German ch-sounds (ich-laut vs. ach-laut):**
- **[ç]** - After front vowels: "ich" (I) **[ɪç]**, "nicht" (not) **[nɪçt]**
- **[x]** - After back vowels: "Bach" **[bax]**, "Nacht" (night) **[naxt]**

**German r-sounds:**
- **[ʁ]** - Uvular fricative: "rot" (red) **[ʁoːt]**, "Bruder" (brother) **[ˈbʁuːdɐ]**
- **[ɐ]** - Vocalized r at end: "Vater" (father) **[ˈfaːtɐ]**, "hier" (here) **[hiːɐ]**

Examples:
- "guten Tag" (good day) **[ˈɡuːtən taːk]**
- "danke" (thanks) **[ˈdaŋkə]**
- "bitte" (please) **[ˈbɪtə]**
- "auf Wiedersehen" (goodbye) **[aʊf ˈviːdɐˌzeːən]**

### Italian Pure Vowels

**Rule:** Italian has 7 vowel sounds (two e's, two o's) and always pronounces every written vowel clearly.

**Italian vowels:**
- **[i]** - "sì" (yes) **[si]**, "vino" (wine) **[ˈvino]**
- **[e]** - closed "e": "sera" (evening) **[ˈseɾa]**, "mela" (apple) **[ˈmela]**
- **[ɛ]** - open "e": "bello" (beautiful) **[ˈbɛllo]**
- **[a]** - "casa" (house) **[ˈkasa]**, "mano" (hand) **[ˈmano]**
- **[ɔ]** - open "o": "cosa" (thing) **[ˈkɔza]**
- **[o]** - closed "o": "nome" (name) **[ˈnome]**
- **[u]** - "uno" (one) **[ˈuno]**, "su" (on) **[su]**

**Italian consonants:**
- **[ʎ]** - "gli": "famiglia" (family) **[faˈmiʎʎa]**, "figlio" (son) **[ˈfiʎʎo]**
- **[ɲ]** - "gn": "gnocchi" **[ˈɲɔkki]**, "ognuno" (everyone) **[oɲˈɲuno]**
- **[ts]**, **[dz]** - "z": "grazie" (thanks) **[ˈɡratsje]**, "zero" **[ˈdzɛro]**

Examples:
- "ciao" (hi/bye) **[ˈtʃao]**
- "buongiorno" (good morning) **[bwonˈdʒorno]**
- "per favore" (please) **[per faˈvore]**
- "arrivederci" (goodbye) **[arriˈvɛdertʃi]**

## Practice Patterns

1. **Spanish vowel purity:** Pronounce Spanish **[a]**, **[e]**, **[i]**, **[o]**, **[u]** (no diphthongs)
2. **Trill vs. tap:** Spanish "perro" **[ˈpero]** vs. "pero" **[ˈpeɾo]**
3. **French nasal vowels:** Distinguish **[ɛ̃]**, **[ɑ̃]**, **[ɔ̃]** in "vin", "sans", "bon"
4. **French uvular r:** Practice **[ʁ]** in "rouge", "Paris", "merci"
5. **German umlauts:** Produce **[y]**, **[ø]** in "über", "schön"
6. **German ich/ach:** **[ç]** after front vowels, **[x]** after back vowels
7. **Italian double consonants:** "notte" **[ˈnɔtte]** vs. "note" **[ˈnote]**

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Spanish **[e]** vs. English **[eɪ]** → Spanish "peso" is **[ˈpeso]**, not **[ˈpeɪso]** (no diphthong!)
- French **[y]** as "yu" → "tu" is **[ty]** (single rounded vowel), not **[tyu]**
- Missing nasal quality → French "bon" is **[bɔ̃]**, not **[bɔn]** (vowel is nasalized)
- German **[ç]** vs. **[x]** → "ich" is **[ɪç]** (palatal), "ach" is **[ax]** (velar) - different sounds!
- Using English **[ɹ]** → French/German use uvular **[ʁ]**, Spanish uses tap **[ɾ]** or trill **[r]**
- Italian **[ʎ]** as "li" → "famiglia" has single palatal **[ʎ]**, not **[l]** + **[i]**
- Spanish **[x]** as "h" → "jamón" is **[xaˈmon]** (velar fricative), stronger than English "h"
- French **[ø]** vs. **[œ]** → "peu" is **[pø]** (close-mid), "peur" is **[pœʁ]** (open-mid)
- German **[y]** vs. **[ʏ]** → "fühlen" is **[ˈfyːlən]** (long close), "füllen" is **[ˈfʏlən]** (short near-close)
- Not recognizing silent letters → French "Paris" is **[paˈʁi]**, silent final "s"

## Cultural Notes

- Spanish has only 5 vowel sounds while English has 12+ - much simpler vowel system
- French nasal vowels developed when nasal consonants were lost historically
- The uvular **[ʁ]** replaced trilled **[r]** in French and Standard German during the 17th-19th centuries
- Italian maintains Latin's clear vowel system and always pronounces written vowels
- Spanish "r" vs. "rr" distinction is crucial: "pero" (but) vs. "perro" (dog) are different words!
- German capitalization rules affect pronunciation transcription conventions
- French silent final consonants create pronunciation challenges for learners
