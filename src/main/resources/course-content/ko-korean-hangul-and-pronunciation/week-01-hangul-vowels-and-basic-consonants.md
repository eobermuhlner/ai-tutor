## Grammar Focus

### Hangul Syllabary Structure

**Rule:** Hangul consists of consonants (자음 - jaeum) and vowels (모음 - moeum) that combine to form syllable blocks.

### Basic Vowels (모음 - Moeum)
**Rule:** The 10 basic vowels form the foundation of all Hangul syllables.

| Hangul | Romanization | Pronunciation |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| **ㅏ** | a | a as in "father" (open sound) |
| **ㅑ** | ya | ya as in "yard" (longer version of ㅏ) |
| **ㅓ** | eo | eo sound (similar to "uh" but more open) |
| **ㅕ** | yeo | yeo sound (longer version of ㅓ) |
| **ㅗ** | o | o as in "go" (rounded) |
| **ㅛ** | yo | yo as in "yonder" (longer version of ㅗ) |
| **ㅜ** | u | u as in "pool" (rounded) |
| **ㅠ** | yu | yu as in "you" (longer version of ㅜ) |
| **ㅡ** | eu | eu sound (neutral vowel, like French "eux") |
| **ㅣ** | i | i as in "machine" (close sound) |

### Basic Consonants (자음 - Jaeum)
**Rule:** These consonants combine with vowels to form syllable blocks.

| Hangul | Romanization | Sound | English example |
|--------|--------------|-------|-----------------|
| **ㄱ** | g/k | g as in "go" or k as in "ski" (context-dependent) | gate, sky |
| **ㄴ** | n | n as in "no" | nest |
| **ㄷ** | d/t | d as in "do" or t as in "stop" (context-dependent) | dog, stop |
| **ㄹ** | r/l | r between vowels, l at beginning/end | run, milk |
| **ㅁ** | m | m as in "mom" | mother |
| **ㅂ** | b/p | b as in "boy" or p as in "spin" (context-dependent) | boy, spin |
| **ㅅ** | s | s as in "see" | see |
| **ㅇ** | ∅/ng | silent at beginning, ng as in "sing" at end | (silent in "안"), sing |
| **ㅈ** | j/ch | j as in "jump" or ch as in "church" (context-dependent) | jump, church |
| **ㅊ** | ch | ch as in "church" (aspirated) | church |
| **ㅋ** | k | k as in "ski" (aspirated) | sky |
| **ㅌ** | t | t as in "stop" (aspirated) | top |
| **ㅍ** | p | p as in "spin" (aspirated) | pop |
| **ㅎ** | h | h as in "hat" | hat |

### Syllable Block Formation
**Rule:** Korean syllables are formed by combining consonants and vowels in blocks.

**Structure Pattern:**
1. **CV (Consonant-Vowel):** 가 (ga), 도 (do), 수 (su)

**Block Construction Rules:**
- Consonant + Vowel = vertical or horizontal arrangement
- Vertical vowels (ㅏ, ㅑ, ㅓ, ㅕ, ㅣ) = consonant to left of vowel
- Horizontal vowels (ㅗ, ㅛ, ㅜ, ㅠ, ㅡ) = consonant above vowel

**Examples:**
- **가** = ㄱ + ㅏ (vertical arrangement)
- **고** = ㄱ + ㅗ (horizontal arrangement)
- **나** = ㄴ + ㅏ (vertical arrangement)
- **도** = ㄷ + ㅗ (horizontal arrangement)

### Pronunciation Guide
**Key Points:**
1. Each syllable gets equal stress and timing
2. The consonant sound blends directly into the vowel sound
3. ㅓ and ㅡ are distinctive sounds not found in English
4. ㄹ pronunciation varies based on position (r between vowels, l at beginning/end)

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Wrong form → Correct form (explanation)
- **ㅏ (a)** sound → should be pure "a" as in "father" ✓
- **ㅓ (eo)** sound → unique Korean sound, similar to relaxed "uh" ✓
- **ㅡ (eu)** sound → neutral sound, similar to French "eux" ✓
- **ㅣ (i)** sound → like "i" in "machine", not "hi" ✓
- **ㄱ (g)** → not "ge" (just the consonant sound) ✓
- **ㄴ (n)** → not "en" (just the consonant sound) ✓
- **ㅂ (b)** → not "be" (just the consonant sound) ✓
- Confusing vertical vowels (ㅏ, ㅑ, ㅓ, ㅕ, ㅣ) with horizontal ones ✓
- Confusing horizontal vowels (ㅗ, ㅛ, ㅜ, ㅠ, ㅡ) with vertical ones ✓

## Cultural Notes

- Hangul is considered one of the world's most scientifically designed writing systems
- Hangul was created in the 15th century by King Sejong and is celebrated as a cultural treasure
- Korean people take pride in their writing system and appreciate when foreigners make the effort to learn it
- Handwriting quality is important in Korean culture
- Learning Hangul shows respect for Korean culture and language
- The scientific design of Hangul makes it possible to learn the basics relatively quickly
- Most Koreans are patient with pronunciation mistakes from learners
- Understanding Hangul is essential before attempting to learn Korean grammar and vocabulary
