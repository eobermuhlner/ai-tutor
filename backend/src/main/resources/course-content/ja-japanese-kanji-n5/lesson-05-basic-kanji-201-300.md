## Lesson Goals

- Learn kanji related to home and housing
- Master vocabulary for rooms and furniture
- Understand animal names and nature terms
- Practice plant and tree vocabulary

## Grammar Focus

**Minimal grammar**: Focus on recognizing and reading kanji rather than complex grammatical structures.

**Basic expressions**:
- **Home**: **家 (いえ)**, **部屋 (へや)**, **家族 (かぞく)**
- **Animals**: **犬 (いぬ)**, **猫 (ねこ)**, **鳥 (とり)**

Examples:
- **家にいます** - Ie ni imasu (I am at home)
- **部屋を掃除します** - Heya wo souji shimasu (I clean the room)

## Conversation Scenarios

Practice using kanji related to home, animals, and nature. The tutor will help you use these characters in context.

Topics to cover:
- Describing your home and rooms
- Talking about pets and animals
- Discussing plants and gardens
- Expressing preferences for living spaces

Tutor will prompt: "Can you describe your home using these kanji?" or "What's your favorite animal and why?"

## Practice Patterns

1. Write each kanji 3 times with correct stroke order
2. Create sentences about home and housing
3. Practice describing rooms and furniture
4. Use animal vocabulary in context
5. Express preferences about plants and nature
6. Practice reading simple phrases with the new characters

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- **Confusing similar kanji** → 犬 (dog) vs 猫 (cat) - different animals
- **Using wrong readings** → 花 (はな/か) vs 葉 (は/よう) - different meanings
- **Forgetting context for readings** → Some kanji change meaning in compounds
- **Mixing up similar meanings** → 庭 (garden) vs 畳 (tatami) - different parts of house
- **Stroke order errors** → Plant kanji often have complex strokes

## Cultural Notes

- Traditional Japanese homes feature tatami mat flooring and sliding doors
- Many Japanese homes have small gardens or planters
- Pets are often kept indoors, especially cats and small dogs
- Cherry blossoms are culturally significant in Japan with "hanami" viewing
- Many traditional Japanese rooms are multi-purpose and flexible
- Japanese bathrooms traditionally have separate areas for bathing and washing
