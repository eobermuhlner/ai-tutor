## Lesson Goals

- Comprehensive review of all 800+ kanji learned
- Practice reading comprehension with mixed kanji and kana
- Apply kanji knowledge in practical contexts
- Prepare for advancement to next level

## Grammar Focus

**Minimal grammar**: Focus on recognizing and reading kanji rather than complex grammatical structures.

**Review expressions**:
- **All previously learned vocabulary** with emphasis on correct kanji usage
- **Sentence patterns** incorporating multiple kanji
- **Reading comprehension** with natural language flow

Examples:
- **毎朝本を読んでいます** - Maiasa hon wo yonde imasu (I read books every morning)
- **家族と一緒にお祝いしました** - Kazoku to issho ni oiwai shimashita (I celebrated with my family)

## Conversation Scenarios

Apply all learned kanji in comprehensive conversation scenarios. The tutor will help integrate multiple concepts and ensure proper usage in context.

Topics to cover:
- Describing a typical day using time and activity kanji
- Discussing family relationships and daily activities
- Talking about work, school, or study experiences
- Sharing experiences about shopping, eating, or traveling

Tutor will prompt: "Tell me about your daily routine using as many kanji as possible" or "Describe your family and your daily activities."

## Practice Patterns

1. Create complex sentences combining kanji from different categories
2. Read and comprehend longer passages with mixed kanji and kana
3. Practice writing short paragraphs using target vocabulary
4. Apply kanji in practical conversation contexts
5. Review stroke order for challenging characters
6. Connect kanji to real-life situations and experiences

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- **Mixed readings** → Ensure consistency in on'yomi/kun'yomi usage
- **Context-appropriate kanji** → Some words require specific kanji in certain contexts
- **Particle confusion** → Kanji need appropriate particles for grammatical correctness
- **Stroke order errors** → Complex kanji require proper formation
- **Homophone confusion** → Same reading, different meanings (e.g., 見る vs 聞く)

## Cultural Notes

- Kanji literacy is fundamental to understanding Japanese newspapers, signs, and formal documents
- The ability to read and write kanji is essential for academic and professional success in Japan
- Kanji knowledge reflects cultural literacy and understanding of traditional concepts
- Regular review and practice are crucial for maintaining kanji retention
- Understanding radical components helps with recognizing and remembering new kanji
- Context and surrounding text often indicate the correct reading of ambiguous kanji
