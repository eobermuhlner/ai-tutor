## Lesson Goals

- Learn fundamental kanji components and radicals
- Understand on'yomi (Chinese reading) vs kun'yomi (Japanese reading)
- Master kanji for daily activities and objects
- Build foundation for more complex characters

## Grammar Focus

**Minimal grammar**: Focus on recognizing and reading kanji rather than complex grammatical structures.

**Basic verb patterns**:
- **する (suru)** - to do
- **ある (aru)** - to exist (inanimate objects)
- **いる (iru)** - to exist (animate beings/living things)

Examples:
- **勉強する** - Benkyou suru (to study)
- **本がある** - Hon ga aru (There is a book)
- **猫がいる** - Neko ga iru (There is a cat)

## Conversation Scenarios

Practice using kanji in common daily expressions and activities. The tutor will help you recognize and use these characters in context.

Topics to cover:
- Talking about daily activities
- Expressing time and dates
- Describing family members
- Discussing basic needs and wants

Tutor will prompt: "Can you write this with kanji?" or "How would you say this in Japanese?"

## Practice Patterns

1. Write each kanji 3 times with correct stroke order
2. Distinguish between on'yomi and kun'yomi readings
3. Create simple sentences using the new kanji
4. Match kanji to their meanings
5. Practice reading simple phrases with the new characters
6. Connect kanji to daily life situations

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- **Confusing similar readings** → 見る (miru) vs 聞く (kiku) - different actions!
- **Using wrong reading** → Remember context determines on'yomi/kun'yomi
- **Mixing up radicals** → Pay attention to visual components
- **Forgetting stroke order** → Always follow standard stroke principles
- **Incorrect kanji usage** → Some words have specific kanji forms

## Cultural Notes

- Kanji readings can vary based on context and compounds
- Understanding radicals helps with reading unfamiliar characters
- Kanji are used for content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives) while hiragana is used for grammatical elements
- Japanese people learn kanji in specific grade levels in school
- Many kanji have both Chinese-derived (on'yomi) and Japanese (kun'yomi) readings
- Kanji simplification occurred in Japan after WWII to improve literacy
