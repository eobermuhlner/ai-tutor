## Grammar Focus

**Minimal grammar**: Focus on recognizing and reading kanji rather than complex grammatical structures.

**Review expressions**:
- **Review all previous vocabulary** with emphasis on proper kanji usage
- **Sentence construction** using multiple kanji from different categories

Examples:
- **本を読んでいます** - Hon wo yonde imasu (I am reading a book)
- **学校に行きます** - Gakkou ni ikimasu (I go to school)

## Practice Patterns

1. Write complex sentences using multiple kanji from different categories
2. Read passages containing mixed kanji and kana
3. Practice proper stroke order for difficult characters
4. Connect vocabulary items to create meaningful sentences
5. Use time expressions and particles with kanji
6. Practice reading aloud with correct pronunciation

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- **Confusing similar kanji** → 日 (day/sun) vs 曰 (say) - different meanings
- **Using wrong readings** → Context determines on'yomi vs kun'yomi readings
- **Forgetting particles** → Kanji need appropriate particles to form complete sentences
- **Mixing up similar concepts** → Ensure proper usage of related terms
- **Stroke order errors** → Review proper stroke order for all kanji

## Cultural Notes

- Reading Japanese text requires understanding the appropriate reading (on'yomi/kun'yomi) based on context
- Kanji are often used for content words while hiragana is used for grammatical elements
- The ability to read and write kanji is fundamental to literacy in Japanese
- Regular practice and review are essential for kanji retention
- Understanding radicals helps with recognizing and remembering kanji
- Japanese people learn kanji in a specific order, starting with the most basic and common ones
