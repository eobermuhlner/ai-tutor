## Lesson Goals

- Learn kanji related to shopping and money
- Master commercial vocabulary
- Understand colors and descriptive adjectives
- Practice expressing emotions and feelings

## Grammar Focus

**Minimal grammar**: Focus on recognizing and reading kanji rather than complex grammatical structures.

**Basic expressions**:
- **Money**: **お金 (おかね)**, **円 (えん)**, **払う (はらう)**
- **Shopping**: **買う (かう)**, **売る (うる)**, **値段 (ねだん)**

Examples:
- **お金を払います** - Okane wo haraimasu (I pay money)
- **物を買います** - Mono wo kaimasu (I buy things)

## Conversation Scenarios

Practice using kanji related to shopping, money, colors, emotions, and adjectives. The tutor will help you use these characters in context.

Topics to cover:
- Shopping for items and asking for prices
- Discussing colors and describing objects
- Expressing emotions and feelings
- Handling money and making payments

Tutor will prompt: "Can you ask for the price of this item?" or "How do you express that you like something?"

## Practice Patterns

1. Write each kanji 3 times with correct stroke order
2. Create sentences about shopping and money
3. Practice describing objects with colors and adjectives
4. Express emotions and feelings using kanji
5. Use commercial vocabulary in context
6. Practice reading simple phrases with the new characters

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- **Confusing similar kanji** → 色 (color) vs 情 (emotion) - different meanings
- **Using wrong readings** → 赤 (あか/せき) vs 青 (あお/せい) - be careful with readings
- **Forgetting context for readings** → Some kanji change meaning in compounds
- **Mixing up adjectives** → 良 (good) vs 悪 (bad) - opposite meanings
- **Stroke order errors** → Emotion kanji often have complex strokes

## Cultural Notes

- Cash is still widely used in Japan, though digital payments are increasing
- Customer service in Japan is known for exceptional attention to detail
- Color symbolism in Japan differs from other cultures (e.g., white represents purity)
- Expressing emotions is often more subtle in Japanese culture
- Gift-giving culture in Japan places importance on presentation and wrapping
- Japanese has different levels of politeness that affect shopping conversations
