## Grammar Focus

### The Verb "Gustar" (To Be Pleasing To)

**Rule:** "Gustar" means "to be pleasing to" - the thing liked is the subject, the person is the indirect object

Structure: A mí + me + gusta + [singular noun]
         A ti + te + gusta + [singular noun]

Note: The noun is what is liked, not what likes

Examples:
- Me gusta el café (Coffee is pleasing to me / I like coffee)
- Me gusta la sopa (Soup is pleasing to me / I like soup)
- No me gusta el arroz (Rice is not pleasing to me / I don't like rice)

Patterns to Practice:
- Use "me gusta" with singular nouns
- Use "no me gusta" to express dislike
- "A mí" is optional but can be used for emphasis

---

## Vocabulary

- **Me gusta** - I like (literally "it is pleasing to me")
- **No me gusta** - I don't like
- **¿Te gusta?** - Do you like?
- **¿A ti te gusta?** - Do you like?

The AI tutor will guide the learner through expressing food preferences.

Practice areas:

- The learner expressing negative preferences
- The learner asking others about their preferences

---

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Thinking "gustar" means "to like" like English verbs
- Forgetting to use the indirect object pronoun (me, te, etc.)

---

## Practice Patterns

- The learner expresses likes: "Me gusta el café"
- The learner expresses dislikes: "No me gusta la sopa"
- The learner asks others: "¿Te gusta el pescado?"
