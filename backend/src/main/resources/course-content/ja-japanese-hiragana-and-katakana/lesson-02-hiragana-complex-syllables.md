- Understand small ゃゅょ combinations
- Practice all hiragana characters together

## Grammar Focus

### H-column (h- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "h" with each vowel. Note that ふ is pronounced "fu" not "hu".

| Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **は** | ha | hah (particle "wa" when used as particle) |
| **ひ** | hi | hee |
| **ふ** | fu | fu (similar to "hoo" but with less air) |
| **へ** | he | heh (particle "e" when used as particle) |
| **ほ** | ho | hoh |

### M-column (m- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "m" with each vowel.

| Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ま** | ma | mah |
| **み** | mi | mee |
| **む** | mu | moo |
| **め** | me | meh |
| **も** | mo | moh |

### Y-column (y- sounds)
**Rule:** Only three characters exist in this column with full vowels.

| Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **や** | ya | yah |
| **ゆ** | yu | yoo |
| **よ** | yo | yoh |

### R-column (r- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "r" with each vowel (Japanese "r" is between English "r" and "l").

| Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ら** | ra | rah |
| **り** | ri | ree |
| **る** | ru | roo |
| **れ** | re | reh |
| **ろ** | ro | roh |

### W-column (w- sounds)
**Rule:** Only two full vowel characters remain in common use.

| Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **わ** | wa | wah |
| **を** | o | o (used only as object particle, pronounced "o") |

### Special Character: ん (n)
**Rule:** This is the only hiragana that doesn't end in a vowel. It represents a nasal sound.

| Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ん** | n | nasal "n" sound |

### Small ゃ, ゅ, ょ (Yōon)
**Rule:** These small characters combine with certain consonants to create "glided" sounds.

| Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|--------|---------------|
| **きゃ** | kya | kee-yah |
| **きゅ** | kyu | kee-yoo |
| **きょ** | kyo | kee-yoh |
| **しゃ** | sha | shee-yah |
| **しゅ** | shu | shee-yoo |
| **しょ** | sho | shee-yoh |
| **ちゃ** | cha | chee-yah |
| **ちゅ** | chu | chee-yoo |
| **ちょ** | cho | chee-yoh |
| **にゃ** | nya | nee-yah |
| **にゅ** | nyu | nee-yoo |
| **にょ** | nyo | nee-yoh |

## Essential Vocabulary

### Basic Words Using Learned Characters
- **はな (hana)** - flower / nose
- **ひ (hi)** - fire / day
- **ふ (fu)** - wind / 2 (counter)
- **へ (he)** - toward (particle)
- **ほ (ho)** - this (polite)
- **みず (mizu)** - water
- **や (ya)** - house (old) / 8 (when used as number)
- **ゆ (yu)** - hot water / bath
- **よ (yo)** - night (when used as particle)
- **りんご (ringo)** - apple
- **るす (rusu)** - away from home
- **むら (mura)** - village
- **まん (man)** - 10,000
- **ん (n)** - (ending particle)
- **こんにちは (konnichiwa)** - hello

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Wrong form → Correct form (explanation)
- **は (ha)** when particle → **は (wa)** (particle わ is correct) ✓
- **へ (he)** when particle → **へ (e)** (particle え is correct) ✓
- **を (wo)** → **お (o)** (particle を is pronounced "o", not "wo") ✓
- **しょ (sho)** → **そ (so)** (confusing compound sounds with basic ones) ✓
- **ちゃ (cha)** → **て (te)** (mixing up compound and basic sounds) ✓
- **にゅ (nyu)** → **に (ni)** (similar confusion with compounds) ✓
- **ん (n)** → missing or extra sound (special character needs attention) ✓
- **ふ (fu)** → **ふ (hu)** (fu is correct pronunciation) ✓
- **り (ri)** → **る (ru)** (confusing similar-looking characters) ✓
- **わ (wa)** → **う (u)** (confusing characters) ✓

## Cultural Notes

- The ん character is unique and important in Japanese phonology
- Small ゃゅょ combinations are common in loanwords and create the "y" gliding sound
- Proper pronunciation of ん is important as it can change word meaning
- Stroke order is especially important for compound characters
- Japanese people often use hiragana for grammatical particles and native words
- The hiragana script was developed from simplified cursive Chinese characters (manyōgana)
- Reading fluency in hiragana is essential before learning kanji
