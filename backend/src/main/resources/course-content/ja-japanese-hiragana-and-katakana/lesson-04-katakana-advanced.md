## Lesson Goals

- Master remaining katakana consonant sounds (h, m, y, r, w columns)
- Learn compound sounds (small ィュョ)
- Understand long vowel and double consonant marks
- Practice reading complex loanwords

## Grammar Focus

### H-column (h- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "h" with each vowel. Note that フ is pronounced "fu" not "hu".

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ハ** | は | ha | hah (particle "wa" when used as particle) |
| **ヒ** | ひ | hi | hee |
| **フ** | ふ | fu | fu (similar to "hoo" but with less air) |
| **ヘ** | へ | he | heh (particle "e" when used as particle) |
| **ホ** | ほ | ho | hoh |

### M-column (m- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "m" with each vowel.

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **マ** | ま | ma | mah |
| **ミ** | み | mi | mee |
| **ム** | む | mu | moo |
| **メ** | め | me | meh |
| **モ** | も | mo | moh |

### Y-column (y- sounds)
**Rule:** Only three characters exist in this column with full vowels.

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ヤ** | や | ya | yah |
| **ユ** | ゆ | yu | yoo |
| **ヨ** | よ | yo | yoh |

### R-column (r- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "r" with each vowel (Japanese "r" is between English "r" and "l").

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ラ** | ら | ra | rah |
| **リ** | り | ri | ree |
| **ル** | る | ru | roo |
| **レ** | れ | re | reh |
| **ロ** | ろ | ro | roh |

### W-column (w- sounds)
**Rule:** Only two full vowel characters remain in common use.

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ワ** | わ | wa | wah |
| **ヲ** | を | o | o (used only as object particle, pronounced "o") |

### Special Character: ン (n)
**Rule:** This is the only katakana that doesn't end in a vowel. It represents a nasal sound.

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ン** | ん | n | nasal "n" sound |

### Small ィ, ュ, ョ (Yōon - Compound Sounds)
**Rule:** These small characters combine with certain consonants to create "glided" sounds.

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **キャ** | きゃ | kya | kee-yah |
| **キュ** | きゅ | kyu | kee-yoo |
| **キョ** | きょ | kyo | kee-yoh |
| **シャ** | しゃ | sha | shee-yah |
| **シュ** | しゅ | shu | shee-yoo |
| **ショ** | しょ | sho | shee-yoh |
| **チャ** | ちゃ | cha | chee-yah |
| **チュ** | ちゅ | chu | chee-yoo |
| **チョ** | ちょ | cho | chee-yoh |
| **ニャ** | にゃ | nya | nee-yah |
| **ニュ** | にゅ | nyu | nee-yoo |
| **ニョ** | にょ | nyo | nee-yoh |

### Long Vowel Mark (ー)
**Rule:** The dash (ー) extends the vowel sound of the previous character.

| Katakana | Romaji | Meaning |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **カー (kaa)** | kaa | car |
| **コーヒー (ko-hii)** | ko-hii | coffee |
| **パーティー (pa-ti-)** | pa-ti- | party |
| **メール (me-ru)** | me-ru | email |

### Double Consonant Mark (ッ)
**Rule:** The small ッ represents a pause before a consonant sound (sokuon).

| Katakana | Romaji | Meaning |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **カップ (kappu)** | kappu | cup |
| **サッカー (sakkaa)** | sakkaa | soccer |
| **カップル (kappuru)** | kappuru | couple |

- Read all katakana characters from memory
- Practice compound sounds and their hiragana equivalents
- Identify long vowels and double consonants in loanwords
- Match katakana to their original foreign words when possible
- Distinguish between similar-looking katakana characters

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Wrong form → Correct form (explanation)
- **フ (fu)** → **ハ (ha)** (confusing similar starting strokes) ✓
- **チ (chi)** → **ヒ (hi)** (confusing characters) ✓
- **ツ (tsu)** → **ソ (so)** (confusing shapes) ✓
- **レ (re)** → **カ (ka)** (confusing with hiragana ろ) ✓
- **ォ (o)** → **エ (e)** (confusing small characters) ✓
- **ッ (sokuon)** → missing the pause sound ✓
- **ー (chōon)** → not extending the vowel sound ✓
- **キャ (kya)** → **カ (ka)** (confusing with basic characters) ✓
- **ショー (shoo)** → **ショ (sho)** (missing long vowel) ✓

## Cultural Notes

- Many modern Japanese words come from English, Portuguese, Dutch, German and French
- Japanese adapts foreign sounds to fit the Japanese phonetic system
- Long vowel marks are essential for proper pronunciation of loanwords
- Double consonant marks add emphasis and timing to loanwords
- Japanese people often use katakana for new technology and modern terms
- Some loanwords have meanings that differ from their original language
- Katakana is sometimes used in manga and anime for visual effect or emphasis
- Learning katakana helps understand the international influence on modern Japanese
