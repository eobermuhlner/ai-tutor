## Grammar Focus

### What is Katakana?

**Rule:** Katakana (片仮名) is one of the three Japanese writing systems. It has the same 46 basic sounds as hiragana but different visual forms.

**Purpose:** Katakana is used primarily for:
- Foreign loanwords (コーヒー - coffee)
- Onomatopoeia (ドキドキ - heartbeat sound)
- Emphasis or stylistic effect
- Scientific terms
- Names of foreign countries, people, and places

### Basic Katakana Structure

Katakana characters are more angular and simplified compared to hiragana. Each character represents the same syllable sounds as hiragana.

### Vowel Sounds (ア-オ)
**Rule:** The five basic vowels have simple, angular katakana forms.

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ア** | あ | a | a as in "father" (short) |
| **イ** | い | i | i as in "machine" (short) |
| **ウ** | う | u | u as in "rule" (short) |
| **エ** | え | e | e as in "bed" (short) |
| **オ** | お | o | o as in "go" (short) |

### K-column (k- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "k" with each vowel in angular katakana forms.

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **カ** | か | ka | kah |
| **キ** | き | ki | key |
| **ク** | く | ku | koo |
| **ケ** | け | ke | keh |
| **コ** | こ | ko | koh |

### S-column (s- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "s" with each vowel. **シ** is pronounced "shi", **ツ** is pronounced "tsu".

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **サ** | さ | sa | sah |
| **シ** | し | shi | shee |
| **ス** | す | su | soo |
| **セ** | せ | se | seh |
| **ソ** | そ | so | soh |

### T-column (t- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "t" with each vowel. **チ** is pronounced "chi", **ツ** is pronounced "tsu".

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **タ** | た | ta | tah |
| **チ** | ち | chi | chee |
| **ツ** | つ | tsu | tsu (like "s" + "oo") |
| **テ** | て | te | teh |
| **ト** | と | to | toh |

### N-column (n- sounds)
**Rule:** These characters combine the consonant "n" with each vowel.

| Katakana | Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation |
|----------|----------|--------|---------------|
| **ナ** | な | na | nah |
| **ニ** | に | ni | nee |
| **ヌ** | ぬ | nu | noo |
| **ネ** | ね | ne | neh |
| **ノ** | の | no | noh |

### Comparison with Hiragana
**Rule:** Each katakana character corresponds to exactly one hiragana character with the same sound.

- Katakana: Angular, simpler strokes
- Hiragana: Cursive, flowing strokes
- Same pronunciation and function
- Different usage contexts

## Cultural Notes

- Katakana was developed from parts (components) of Chinese characters
- Katakana is often used in advertising and signage for visual impact
- Japanese people switch between hiragana, katakana, and kanji naturally in text
- Loanwords in katakana may be pronounced differently than in their origin language
- Katakana is used for emphasis similar to italics in English text
- Learning katakana well helps with understanding modern Japanese vocabulary which contains many loanwords
- Japanese people often use katakana to write names of foreign people and places
