## Lesson Goals

- Communicate symptoms and health issues clearly
- Request over-the-counter medications at pharmacies
- Understand dosage and usage instructions
- Ask for medical help when needed
- Handle prescriptions and insurance

## Grammar Focus

### Describing Pain and Symptoms

**Rule:** Use "Me duele..." (singular) or "Me duelen..." (plural) to say something hurts. "Tengo..." (I have...) for symptoms.

Examples:
- "Me duele la cabeza" (My head hurts / I have a headache)
- "Me duelen los oídos" (My ears hurt)
- "Tengo fiebre" (I have a fever)
- "Tengo tos" (I have a cough)

### Asking for Medication

**Rule:** Use "Necesito algo para..." (I need something for...) to request medication.

Examples:
- "Necesito algo para el dolor de cabeza" (I need something for a headache)
- "Necesito algo para la tos" (I need something for a cough)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Buying Medicine at the Pharmacy

Practice requesting over-the-counter medication for common ailments. Describe your symptoms and ask for recommendations.

Essential phrases:
- "Tengo dolor de cabeza" (I have a headache)
- "Necesito algo para el dolor de estómago" (I need something for a stomachache)
- "¿Tiene algo para la alergia?" (Do you have something for allergies?)
- "Sin receta" (Over-the-counter)
- "¿Cómo se toma?" (How do I take it?)

### Scenario 2: Understanding Medication Instructions

Practice asking about and understanding how to take medication properly. Confirm dosage, frequency, and any precautions.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Cuántas veces al día?" (How many times per day?)
- "¿Antes o después de comer?" (Before or after eating?)
- "¿Cuántas pastillas?" (How many pills?)
- "¿Puedo tomarlo con otros medicamentos?" (Can I take it with other medications?)

### Scenario 3: Describing Symptoms to Get Help

Practice describing your symptoms to determine if you need to see a doctor or can self-treat with pharmacy medication.

Essential phrases:
- "Me duele mucho la garganta" (My throat hurts a lot)
- "Tengo fiebre desde ayer" (I've had a fever since yesterday)
- "También tengo tos" (I also have a cough)
- "¿Necesito ver un médico?" (Do I need to see a doctor?)

### Scenario 4: Handling a Prescription

Practice filling a prescription at the pharmacy. Present your prescription, ask about cost, and confirm insurance coverage.

Essential phrases:
- "Tengo una receta" (I have a prescription)
- "¿Cuánto cuesta?" (How much does it cost?)
- "Tengo seguro médico" (I have medical insurance)
- "¿Lo cubre el seguro?" (Does insurance cover it?)
- "¿Cuándo estará listo?" (When will it be ready?)

## Practice Patterns

- Describe symptoms accurately to get appropriate medication
- Request and receive over-the-counter medication with instructions
- Understand and follow dosage instructions correctly
- Communicate allergies and existing medical conditions
- Fill a prescription and handle payment/insurance

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- "Me duele los oídos" → "Me duelen los oídos" (use plural "duelen" for plural body parts)
- "Tengo un dolor de cabeza" → "Tengo dolor de cabeza" or "Me duele la cabeza" (no article needed)
- "Para dolor" → "Para el dolor" (need article "el")
- Not mentioning allergies → Always tell pharmacist "Soy alérgico a..." if relevant!
- "Necesito medicina" → "Necesito algo para..." (be specific about what for)
- Confusing "receta" (prescription) with "recibo" (receipt) → Different words!

## Cultural Notes

- **Pharmacies are medical advisors**: Spanish pharmacists give medical advice and recommend treatments. They're highly trained and trusted for minor ailments.
- **Green cross sign**: Look for green illuminated cross to identify pharmacies. Some flash to show they're open.
- **Prescription requirements**: Many medications require prescriptions in Spain that may not elsewhere (antibiotics, strong painkillers). Pharmacist can't sell without prescription.
- **Late-night pharmacies**: "Farmacia de guardia" operates 24 hours on rotating schedule. List posted on pharmacy doors and online.
- **Healthcare system**: Spain has excellent public healthcare. EU citizens can use European Health Card. Non-EU should have travel insurance.
- **Emergency number**: 112 is the universal emergency number in Spain (like 911). Operators speak English.
- **Pharmacies sell limited items**: Spanish pharmacies primarily sell medications, not general items like US drugstores. For toiletries, go to "droguería."
- **No self-service**: Can't browse and select medications yourself. Must ask pharmacist, who retrieves items from behind counter.
- **Generic medications**: Ask for "genérico" for cheaper generic version of brand-name drugs. Quality equivalent, much cheaper.
- **Over-the-counter availability**: Some medications available without prescription in other countries require prescription in Spain (codeine, strong antihistamines, etc.).
