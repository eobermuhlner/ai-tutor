## This Week's Goals

- Hail a taxi and communicate your destination

- Confirm the fare and payment method

- Use ride-sharing apps in Spanish-speaking contexts

## Grammar Focus

### Giving Destinations and Directions

**Rule:** Use "A..." (To...) for destinations: "Al aeropuerto" (To the airport), "A la estación" (To the station).

Examples:
- "Al hotel Ritz, por favor" (To the Ritz hotel, please)
- "A la calle Mayor número 15" (To Mayor street number 15)
- "Al centro, por favor" (To downtown, please)

### Imperative for Directions

**Rule:** Simple commands for directions: "Pare" (Stop), "Siga" (Continue), "Gire" (Turn).

Examples:
- "Pare aquí, por favor" (Stop here, please)
- "Siga todo recto" (Continue straight ahead)
- "Gire a la derecha" (Turn right)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Taking a Taxi from the Airport

Practice hailing a taxi at the airport and communicating your hotel destination. Confirm the route and ask about fare.

Essential phrases:
- "Al Hotel Princesa, por favor" (To Hotel Princesa, please)
- "Está en la calle Serrano" (It's on Serrano street)
- "¿Cuánto cuesta aproximadamente?" (How much does it cost approximately?)
- "¿Cuánto tiempo tarda?" (How long does it take?)
- "¿Puede abrir el maletero?" (Can you open the trunk?)

### Scenario 2: Giving Directions During the Ride

Practice providing navigation assistance when the driver doesn't know the exact location. Give turn-by-turn directions.

Essential phrases:
- "Siga todo recto" (Continue straight)
- "Gire a la derecha en el semáforo" (Turn right at the traffic light)
- "Es la próxima calle a la izquierda" (It's the next street on the left)
- "Pare aquí, por favor" (Stop here, please)
- "Es este edificio" (It's this building)

### Scenario 3: Confirming an Uber/Ride-Share

Practice confirming your Uber or Cabify ride. Verify driver name, license plate, and provide your name as passenger.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Es usted Carlos?" (Are you Carlos?)
- "Mi nombre es Jennifer" (My name is Jennifer)
- "El código es 1234" (The code is 1234)
- "Al aeropuerto, terminal 1" (To the airport, terminal 1)

### Scenario 4: Handling Payment and Tipping

Practice paying for your taxi ride. Confirm the total, ask about payment methods, and handle change or tipping.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Cuánto es?" (How much is it?)
- "¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?" (Can I pay by card?)
- "Quédese con el cambio" (Keep the change)
- "El recibo, por favor" (The receipt, please)

## Practice Patterns

- Successfully hail a taxi and communicate destination clearly
- Navigate to a location by giving turn-by-turn directions
- Confirm and board an Uber/Cabify ride safely
- Handle payment including cash, card, and tipping
- Request a stop mid-journey and communicate timing

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- "Para el aeropuerto" → "Al aeropuerto" (use "a/al" for destinations, not "para")
- "Stop" in English → Use "Pare" not English words
- Not confirming fare beforehand → Always ask "¿Cuánto cuesta?" before starting, especially no meter
- "A la calle de Mayor" → "A la calle Mayor" (no "de" needed)
- Assuming all taxis take card → Always ask "¿Acepta tarjeta?" before getting in
- Not getting a receipt → Say "El recibo, por favor" if you need one for expenses

## Cultural Notes

- **Official taxis**: Look for licensed taxis with taxi light on roof and official markings. Avoid unmarked cars.
- **Taxi stands vs hailing**: In many cities, taxis wait at designated "parada de taxis." Hailing on street also works in urban areas.
- **Meters are standard**: Official taxis use meters (taxímetro). If driver suggests flat rate, politely insist on meter or negotiate price before starting.
- **Airport surcharges**: Extra fees for airport pickups are normal. Ask "¿Cuánto cuesta aproximadamente?" to avoid surprises.
- **Tipping**: Tipping not expected but appreciated. Rounding up or 5-10% for good service is nice. "Quédese con el cambio" (keep the change) is common.
- **Cash vs card**: Not all taxis accept cards. Always ask "¿Acepta tarjeta?" before getting in. Have cash backup.
- **Uber and Cabify**: Available in major Spanish cities (Madrid, Barcelona). Works same as other countries. Legal and safe.
- **Spanish drivers may chat**: Small talk is common. Drivers might ask where you're from or offer local tips. It's friendly, not intrusive.
- **Luggage help**: Drivers usually help with large luggage but not obligated. Saying "gracias" is sufficient, small tip optional.
- **Night and weekend surcharges**: Fares increase at night, weekends, and holidays. This is normal and shown on meter.
