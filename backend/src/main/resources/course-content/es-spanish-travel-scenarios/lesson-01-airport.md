## This Week's Goals

- Successfully check in for your flight and handle luggage
- Navigate airport facilities and find your gate
- Communicate at security checkpoints and customs
- Ask for help when you need it
- Handle common airport situations with confidence

## Grammar Focus

### Question Formation for Travelers

**Rule:** Use "¿Dónde está...?" (Where is...?) for locations and "¿Cuánto/a...?" (How much/many...?) for quantities.

Examples:
- "¿Dónde está la puerta 12?" (Where is gate 12?)
- "¿Cuánto cuesta?" (How much does it cost?)
- "¿Cuántas maletas puedo llevar?" (How many suitcases can I bring?)

### Polite Requests

**Rule:** Start with "¿Puede...?" (Can you...?) or "Necesito..." (I need...) to ask for help politely.

Examples:
- "¿Puede ayudarme?" (Can you help me?)
- "¿Puede repetir?" (Can you repeat?)
- "Necesito ayuda" (I need help)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Check-In Counter

Practice checking in at the airline counter. Present your passport, answer questions about luggage, request your seat preference, and get your boarding pass. Ask which gate your flight departs from.

Essential phrases:
- "Quiero hacer check-in" (I want to check in)
- "Aquí está mi pasaporte" (Here is my passport)
- "Una maleta" (One suitcase)
- "Prefiero ventanilla" (I prefer window)
- "¿De qué puerta sale?" (Which gate does it leave from?)

### Scenario 2: Security Checkpoint

Practice going through security. Ask where it's located, understand instructions about removing items (shoes, laptop, liquids), and request clarification when needed.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Dónde está el control de seguridad?" (Where is security?)
- "¿Puedo llevar esto?" (Can I bring this?)
- "No entiendo" (I don't understand)
- "¿Puede repetir, por favor?" (Can you repeat, please?)

### Scenario 3: Finding Your Gate

Practice locating your boarding gate after security. Ask for directions, check if your flight is on time, and find the waiting area and bathrooms.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Dónde está la puerta 15?" (Where is gate 15?)
- "¿Dónde están los baños?" (Where are the bathrooms?)
- "¿Está retrasado el vuelo?" (Is the flight delayed?)
- "¿Cuándo embarcamos?" (When do we board?)

### Scenario 4: Immigration and Customs

Practice going through immigration and customs when you arrive in Spain. Answer questions about your visit purpose and duration, collect luggage, and declare items if needed.

Essential phrases:
- "Turismo" (Tourism)
- "Una semana" (One week)
- "No tengo nada que declarar" (I have nothing to declare)
- "¿Dónde está la salida?" (Where is the exit?)

## Practice Patterns

- Complete check-in process from greeting staff to receiving boarding pass
- Successfully navigate to your gate using only Spanish directions
- Handle a flight delay: ask about new departure time and gate changes
- Go through immigration and answer standard questions clearly
- Report a problem (lost item, missed connection) and ask for help

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- "¿Dónde es...?" → "¿Dónde está...?" (use "está" for locations - critical for finding places!)
- Saying "pasaporte" too quietly at check-in → Speak clearly so staff can hear you
- Forgetting "disculpe" before questions → "Disculpe, ¿dónde está...?" (politeness matters!)
- "Equipaje de la mano" → "Equipaje de mano" (no article needed)
- Not understanding "fila" (row) vs "asiento" (seat number) → This causes boarding confusion!
- Saying "gracias" only after service → Say "gracias" often, even for small help
- Panic when you don't understand → Calmly say "No entiendo, más despacio por favor"

## Cultural Notes

- **"Disculpe" first, always**: Start every question with "Disculpe" (excuse me). This is not optional in Spanish culture - it shows basic respect and gets much better service.
- **Greet before requesting**: Say "Buenos días" or "Buenas tardes" to staff before asking for anything. Jumping straight to requests seems rude.
- **Airport arrival time**: Arrive 3 hours early for international flights. Spanish airports can have very long check-in and security lines, especially in summer.
- **Boarding is different**: Boarding may be less organized than you expect. People may crowd the gate. Listen for your row number or zone announcement.
- **Speaking English**: Major airports (Madrid, Barcelona) have English-speaking staff, but trying Spanish first shows respect and often gets friendlier service.
- **Security is strict**: Follow all rules exactly (liquids, electronics, shoes). Security staff are professional but firm - don't joke or argue.
- **Keep documents ready**: You'll show your passport and boarding pass many times. Keep them easily accessible, not buried in your bag.
- **Watch screens, not just announcements**: Gate changes and delays may appear on screens before verbal announcements, especially at smaller airports.
- **Immigration is straightforward**: Answer questions simply and honestly. "Turismo" and stating your stay duration is usually sufficient. Don't volunteer extra information.
- **Tipping not expected**: You don't tip airline or airport staff in Spain. A simple "gracias" is appropriate and sufficient.
