## Lesson Goals

- Call for help in emergency situations
- Report lost or stolen items to police
- Get medical help when urgently needed
- Contact your embassy if necessary
- Handle urgent problems with clear communication

## Grammar Focus

### Expressing Urgency

**Rule:** Use "Necesito..." (I need...) and "Es urgente" (It's urgent) to express emergency needs.

Examples:
- "Necesito ayuda" (I need help)
- "Necesito un médico" (I need a doctor)
- "Es una emergencia" (It's an emergency)
- "Es urgente" (It's urgent)

### Reporting Problems

**Rule:** Use "Me han robado..." (They've robbed me...) or "He perdido..." (I've lost...) to report theft or loss.

Examples:
- "Me han robado el pasaporte" (They've stolen my passport)
- "He perdido la cartera" (I've lost my wallet)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Medical Emergency - Calling for Help

Practice calling for medical help in an urgent situation. Clearly state the emergency, location, and what help you need.

Essential phrases:
- "Necesito una ambulancia" (I need an ambulance)
- "Es una emergencia médica" (It's a medical emergency)
- "Estoy en la calle Mayor número 25" (I'm at Mayor street number 25)
- "Una persona está herida" (A person is injured)
- "No respira bien" (Not breathing well)

### Scenario 2: Reporting Theft to Police

Practice reporting a theft or loss to police. Provide details about what was stolen, when, and where it happened.

Essential phrases:
- "Quiero denunciar un robo" (I want to report a theft)
- "Me han robado la cartera y el pasaporte" (They've stolen my wallet and passport)
- "Fue esta mañana en el metro" (It was this morning on the metro)
- "No vi quién fue" (I didn't see who it was)
- "Necesito una copia de la denuncia" (I need a copy of the report)

### Scenario 3: Lost Passport - Embassy Visit

Practice handling a lost passport situation. Contact your embassy, explain the situation, and request emergency documents.

Essential phrases:
- "He perdido mi pasaporte" (I've lost my passport)
- "Soy ciudadana americana" (I'm an American citizen)
- "Necesito documentos de emergencia" (I need emergency documents)
- "Mi vuelo es mañana" (My flight is tomorrow)
- "¿Qué documentos necesito?" (What documents do I need?)

### Scenario 4: Getting Lost and Finding Help

Practice asking for help when lost. Explain you're lost, don't know where you are, and need directions back to your hotel.

Essential phrases:
- "Estoy perdido/a" (I'm lost)
- "No sé dónde estoy" (I don't know where I am)
- "Necesito volver al Hotel Princesa" (I need to get back to Hotel Princesa)
- "¿Puede ayudarme?" (Can you help me?)
- "¿Dónde está la comisaría?" (Where is the police station?)

## Practice Patterns

- Call emergency services (112) and clearly communicate the problem
- Report theft to police with all necessary details
- Navigate embassy procedures for lost passport
- Get help when lost using landmarks and hotel information
- Handle medical emergency including describing symptoms and location

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Not calling 112 immediately → In real emergencies, call first, then worry about Spanish
- "He robado" → "Me han robado" (passive construction - "they've robbed me," not "I've robbed")
- Forgetting to get police report → Essential for insurance claims! Say "Necesito la denuncia"
- Not knowing hotel address → Always carry hotel business card or address written down
- "Es emergency" → "Es una emergencia" (use Spanish word)
- Panic and forget all Spanish → Stay calm, even "No hablo español" gets you English help

## Cultural Notes

- **112 is universal**: Works across all EU countries, including Spain. Operators often speak English. Don't hesitate to call.
- **Police types**: "Policía Nacional" (national police) handles serious crimes. "Policía Local/Municipal" handles local issues. Either can help tourists.
- **Police report essential**: For insurance claims, you MUST have official police report ("denuncia"). Insist on getting copy.
- **Embassy can't pay**: Embassies help with documents but won't give money, pay hotel bills, or get you out of jail. They facilitate, not fund.
- **Emergency passport**: Embassies can issue emergency travel documents (not full passport) to get you home. Bring photos and ID if possible.
- **Credit card theft**: Call your bank immediately. Many have 24-hour international numbers on back of card.
- **Pharmacies for minor issues**: For non-life-threatening issues, 24-hour pharmacy ("farmacia de guardia") faster than ER.
- **Public hospitals**: 112 ambulances take you to public hospitals. EU citizens with health card get free/reduced-cost care. Non-EU: travel insurance essential.
- **Tourist police**: Major cities have tourist police who speak English. Look for "Policía de Turismo" or ask for them.
- **Stay calm**: Spanish emergency services are professional and efficient. Even with language barriers, they'll help. Pointing at translation app or paper works.
