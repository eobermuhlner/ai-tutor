## This Week's Goals

- Successfully check in and out of accommodations
- Request room preferences and ask about amenities
- Report problems with your room
- Ask about hotel services and local recommendations
- Communicate with reception staff confidently

## Grammar Focus

### Making Polite Requests

**Rule:** Use "¿Podría...?" (Could you...?) or "¿Sería posible...?" (Would it be possible...?) for polite requests.

Examples:
- "¿Podría darme otra toalla?" (Could you give me another towel?)
- "¿Sería posible cambiar de habitación?" (Would it be possible to change rooms?)
- "¿Podría llamar un taxi?" (Could you call a taxi?)

### Describing Problems

**Rule:** Use "No funciona..." (doesn't work) or "Hay un problema con..." (There's a problem with...) to report issues.

Examples:
- "No funciona el wifi" (The wifi doesn't work)
- "Hay un problema con la ducha" (There's a problem with the shower)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Checking In

Practice checking in at your hotel or hostel. Confirm your reservation, provide identification, receive your key, and ask about hotel amenities and breakfast times.

Essential phrases:
- "Tengo una reserva a nombre de Smith" (I have a reservation under Smith)
- "Aquí está mi pasaporte" (Here is my passport)
- "¿Cuál es el número de mi habitación?" (What is my room number?)
- "¿Está incluido el desayuno?" (Is breakfast included?)
- "¿Cuál es la contraseña del wifi?" (What is the wifi password?)

### Scenario 2: Requesting Room Preferences

Practice requesting specific room features or changes. Ask for a quiet room, high floor, or different view if available.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Tiene una habitación más tranquila?" (Do you have a quieter room?)
- "Prefiero un piso alto" (I prefer a high floor)
- "¿Sería posible cambiar de habitación?" (Would it be possible to change rooms?)
- "Hay mucho ruido" (There's a lot of noise)

### Scenario 3: Reporting Problems

Practice reporting issues with your room. Describe what's not working and request assistance or replacement items.

Essential phrases:
- "No funciona el aire acondicionado" (The air conditioning doesn't work)
- "No hay agua caliente" (There's no hot water)
- "La ducha está rota" (The shower is broken)
- "Necesito más toallas" (I need more towels)
- "¿Podría enviar a alguien?" (Could you send someone?)

### Scenario 4: Checking Out

Practice checking out of your accommodation. Request your bill, verify charges, arrange luggage storage if needed, and ask for transportation assistance.

Essential phrases:
- "Quiero hacer el check-out" (I want to check out)
- "¿Puedo ver la factura?" (Can I see the bill?)
- "¿Puedo dejar el equipaje hasta las tres?" (Can I leave my luggage until three?)
- "¿Podría llamar un taxi al aeropuerto?" (Could you call a taxi to the airport?)

## Practice Patterns

- Complete check-in process and understand all room and service information
- Successfully request a room change or additional amenities
- Report a problem and get it resolved through Spanish communication
- Ask for local recommendations and understand directions given
- Check out, verify bill, and arrange departure transportation

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- "Habitación para dos personas" → "Habitación doble" (use standard hotel terminology)
- Not saying "a nombre de" → Always say "Tengo una reserva a nombre de..." for clarity
- "La wifi" → "El wifi" (wifi is masculine in Spanish)
- Forgetting to ask about breakfast → "¿Está incluido el desayuno?" - important to know!
- "Quiero check-out" → "Quiero hacer el check-out" (need "hacer")
- Using "password" → Use "contraseña" or "clave" for wifi password

## Cultural Notes

- **Passport required**: Hotels legally must photocopy your passport. This is normal, not suspicious.
- **Check-in times**: Usually 2-3pm. Arriving earlier doesn't guarantee room availability. Call ahead if arriving early.
- **"Hostal" vs "Hotel"**: "Hostal" is a budget hotel (not same as hostel/albergue). "Hotel" is standard hotel. Both are private rooms.
- **Breakfast times**: Spanish breakfast is typically 8-10am. Continental style (coffee, toast, juice). Not large meals.
- **Tipping not expected**: Service charge included. Small tips (1-2 euros) appreciated but not required.
- **Room keys**: Must leave key at reception when leaving hotel in some places. Ask "¿Dejo la llave?" (Do I leave the key?)
- **Quiet hours**: 10pm-8am typically. Noise complaints taken seriously. Spanish culture values sleep.
- **Late check-out**: Not common. If needed, ask early. May incur extra charge.
- **Siesta consideration**: If arriving 2-4pm, reception might be understaffed. Be patient.
- **City tax**: Some cities charge tourist tax (1-3 euros/night). Paid at check-out, not included in online booking price.
