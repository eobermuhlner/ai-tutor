## This Week's Goals

- Buy tickets for museums and attractions
- Understand opening hours and admission policies
- Ask questions during tours and exhibits
- Take photos and follow attraction rules
- Navigate tourist information centers

## Grammar Focus

### Asking About Schedules

**Rule:** Use "¿A qué hora...?" (At what time...?) and "¿Hasta qué hora...?" (Until what time...?) for schedules.

Examples:
- "¿A qué hora abre?" (What time does it open?)
- "¿Hasta qué hora está abierto?" (Until what time is it open?)
- "Abre a las nueve" (It opens at nine)

### Making Requests for Permission

**Rule:** Use "¿Se puede...?" (Can one...?) or "¿Puedo...?" (Can I...?) to ask permission.

Examples:
- "¿Se puede tomar fotos?" (Can one take photos?)
- "¿Puedo entrar con mochila?" (Can I enter with a backpack?)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Buying Museum Tickets

Practice purchasing tickets at a museum or attraction. Ask about ticket types, discounts, and included services.

Essential phrases:
- "Dos entradas, por favor" (Two tickets, please)
- "¿Cuánto cuesta la entrada?" (How much is the ticket?)
- "¿Hay descuento para estudiantes?" (Is there a student discount?)
- "¿Incluye audioguía?" (Does it include audio guide?)
- "¿Hasta qué hora está abierto?" (Until what time is it open?)

### Scenario 2: Joining a Guided Tour

Practice signing up for and participating in a guided tour. Ask about tour times, languages, and duration.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Hay visitas guiadas?" (Are there guided tours?)
- "¿En inglés?" (In English?)
- "¿Cuándo empieza la próxima visita?" (When does the next tour start?)
- "¿Cuánto dura el tour?" (How long does the tour last?)
- "¿Dónde nos encontramos?" (Where do we meet?)

### Scenario 3: Asking Questions at an Attraction

Practice asking informational questions about exhibits, artwork, or historical sites during your visit.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Qué es este edificio?" (What is this building?)
- "¿De qué siglo es?" (What century is it from?)
- "¿Quién fue el arquitecto?" (Who was the architect?)
- "¿Dónde está la obra de Picasso?" (Where is the work by Picasso?)
- "¿En qué sala?" (In which room?)

### Scenario 4: Taking Photos and Following Rules

Practice asking permission to take photos and understanding attraction rules and restrictions.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Se puede tomar fotos?" (Can one take photos?)
- "¿Sin flash?" (Without flash?)
- "¿Me puede hacer una foto aquí?" (Can you take my photo here?)
- "¿Dónde puedo dejar la mochila?" (Where can I leave my backpack?)
- "¿Hay guardarropa?" (Is there a cloakroom?)

## Practice Patterns

- Purchase appropriate tickets with any applicable discounts
- Successfully join a guided tour at scheduled time
- Ask informational questions about exhibits and history
- Understand and follow attraction rules and restrictions
- Navigate attraction using maps and signs

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- "¿Cuándo abre?" → "¿A qué hora abre?" (need "a qué hora" for specific times)
- "Foto" without asking → Always ask "¿Se puede tomar fotos?" before photographing
- Confusing "entrada" (entry/ticket) with "entrada" (entrance door) → Context matters!
- "En qué tiempo fue construido" → "¿Cuándo fue construido?" (use "cuándo" not "qué tiempo")
- Not checking student discount → Always ask "¿Hay descuento para estudiantes?" if applicable
- Touching exhibits without asking → Never assume you can touch, ask first!

## Cultural Notes

- **Siesta closures**: Many museums and attractions close 2-5pm for siesta, especially in smaller cities. Check hours carefully.
- **Monday closures**: Most museums closed Mondays. Plan accordingly. Some exceptions in major tourist cities.
- **Free entry times**: Many museums offer free entry certain days/times (often Sunday mornings or last hours). Check website.
- **Student discounts**: Spanish/EU student cards get discounts. International students: bring student ID, but discount not guaranteed.
- **Senior discounts**: Usually 65+. EU citizens get better discounts than non-EU. Bring ID showing age.
- **Photography rules**: Each site has own rules. Assume flash prohibited in museums. Some ban all photos. Always ask.
- **Dress codes**: Churches and cathedrals require covered shoulders and knees. Bring shawl/pants or denied entry.
- **Audio guides**: Usually available in English, Spanish, and major languages. Small rental fee (3-5 euros) or free with ticket.
- **Lines and booking**: Major attractions (Alhambra, Sagrada Familia) require advance booking. Buy online weeks ahead or risk sold out.
- **"Patrimonio Nacional"**: Spanish royal sites (palaces) offer multi-site pass. Good value if visiting several.
