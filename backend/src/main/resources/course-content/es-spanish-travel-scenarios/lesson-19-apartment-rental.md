## Lesson Goals

- Navigate apartment rental platforms and listings
- Communicate with hosts about check-in
- Understand apartment amenities and rules
- Report problems and request maintenance
- Handle check-out procedures

## Grammar Focus

### Asking About Apartment Features

**Rule:** Use "¿Tiene...?" (Does it have...?) or "¿Hay...?" (Is there...?) to ask about features.

Examples:
- "¿Tiene wifi?" (Does it have wifi?)
- "¿Hay lavadora?" (Is there a washing machine?)
- "¿Cuántas habitaciones tiene?" (How many bedrooms does it have?)

### Reporting Problems

**Rule:** Use "No funciona..." (doesn't work) or "Hay un problema con..." (There's a problem with...).

Examples:
- "No funciona el wifi" (The wifi doesn't work)
- "Hay un problema con la calefacción" (There's a problem with the heating)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Check-In Coordination

Practice coordinating check-in with host. Get key exchange details, wifi password, and apartment instructions.

Essential phrases:
- "Llego a las tres de la tarde" (I arrive at three in the afternoon)
- "¿Dónde nos encontramos para las llaves?" (Where do we meet for the keys?)
- "¿Cuál es la contraseña del wifi?" (What's the wifi password?)
- "¿Cómo funciona la calefacción?" (How does the heating work?)
- "¿Hay instrucciones?" (Are there instructions?)

### Scenario 2: Asking About Amenities

Practice asking host about apartment features and nearby services before or upon arrival.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Tiene lavadora?" (Does it have a washing machine?)
- "¿Hay secador de pelo?" (Is there a hair dryer?)
- "¿Dónde está el supermercado más cercano?" (Where is the nearest supermarket?)
- "¿Hay aparcamiento?" (Is there parking?)

### Scenario 3: Reporting a Problem

Practice reporting issues to host and requesting repairs or assistance.

Essential phrases:
- "No funciona el wifi" (The wifi doesn't work)
- "No hay agua caliente" (There's no hot water)
- "La calefacción no funciona" (The heating doesn't work)
- "¿Puede venir a verlo?" (Can you come look at it?)
- "¿Cuándo puede arreglarlo?" (When can you fix it?)

### Scenario 4: Check-Out Process

Practice completing check-out. Return keys, clarify cleaning expectations, and thank host.

Essential phrases:
- "Me voy mañana a las diez" (I'm leaving tomorrow at ten)
- "¿Dónde dejo las llaves?" (Where do I leave the keys?)
- "¿Tengo que limpiar?" (Do I have to clean?)
- "¿Tengo que sacar la basura?" (Do I have to take out the trash?)
- "Muchas gracias por todo" (Thank you very much for everything)

## Practice Patterns

- Successfully coordinate check-in and obtain keys/codes
- Ask about and locate all apartment amenities
- Report problems clearly and arrange for repairs
- Navigate neighborhood and find necessary services
- Complete check-out following host's instructions

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Not confirming check-in time → Always confirm "¿A qué hora puedo hacer check-in?"
- Assuming amenities exist → Ask specifically "¿Tiene lavadora?" not assume
- "El password del wifi" → "La contraseña del wifi" (use Spanish word)
- Not asking about cleaning → Always clarify "¿Tengo que limpiar?" before check-out
- Waiting too long to report problems → Report immediately so host can fix
- Not reading house rules → Always read "normas de la casa" - avoid problems

## Cultural Notes

- **Airbnb very popular**: Widely used in Spain. Well-regulated in most cities. Good option for longer stays (week+).
- **Check-in flexibility**: Often flexible. Hosts usually accommodate late arrivals. But communicate clearly about your arrival time.
- **Key exchange methods**: Some hosts meet in person. Others leave keys in lockbox with code, or with neighbor, or at nearby office. Confirm method.
- **Cleaning expectations**: Varies by host. Some expect you to clean fully. Others have cleaning service. Ask "¿Tengo que limpiar?" before check-out.
- **Wifi reliability**: Most rentals have wifi but quality varies. Test upon arrival. If problems, report immediately.
- **Noise sensitivity**: Spanish buildings often have thin walls. Neighbors sensitive to noise. Respect quiet hours (usually 10pm-8am).
- **Building entry systems**: Many Spanish buildings have entrance codes or intercoms. Host will explain. Write down code!
- **Longer stays**: Weekly/monthly rentals common. Better rates for longer stays. Many people rent for month+ to live like local.
- **Host communication**: Hosts often very helpful with local recommendations. Ask about restaurants, attractions, transportation.
- **Damage deposit**: Some require deposit (50-200 euros). Refunded after check-out if no damage. Clarify policy.
