## Lesson Goals

- Order meals and drinks confidently in restaurants
- Ask about menu items and ingredients
- Communicate dietary restrictions and allergies
- Request the bill and handle payment
- Navigate different meal times and Spanish dining customs

## Grammar Focus

### Expressing Preferences and Desires

**Rule:** Use "Quiero..." (I want...) or "Me gustaría..." (I would like...) to order. "Me gustaría" is more polite.

Examples:
- "Quiero una cerveza" (I want a beer)
- "Me gustaría el menú del día" (I would like the daily menu)
- "Para mí, la paella" (For me, the paella)

### Asking About Food

**Rule:** Use "¿Qué es...?" (What is...?) and "¿Lleva...?" (Does it have...?) to ask about dishes.

Examples:
- "¿Qué es la tortilla española?" (What is Spanish tortilla?)
- "¿Lleva carne?" (Does it have meat?)
- "¿Tiene gluten?" (Does it have gluten?)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Ordering at a Restaurant

Practice ordering a full meal at a restaurant. Get seated, ask about the menu, order drinks and food, and make special requests.

Essential phrases:
- "Una mesa para dos, por favor" (A table for two, please)
- "La carta, por favor" (The menu, please)
- "¿Qué recomienda?" (What do you recommend?)
- "Para mí, la paella" (For me, the paella)
- "Y para beber, agua sin gas" (And to drink, still water)
- "Sin cebolla, por favor" (Without onion, please)

### Scenario 2: Ordering Coffee and Breakfast

Practice ordering breakfast or coffee at a café. Understand Spanish coffee culture and ordering terminology.

Essential phrases:
- "Un café con leche, por favor" (A coffee with milk, please)
- "¿Tienen tostadas?" (Do you have toast?)
- "Con jamón y tomate" (With ham and tomato)
- "Para llevar" (To go/takeaway)

### Scenario 3: Handling Dietary Restrictions

Practice communicating allergies and dietary needs. Ask about ingredients and find suitable options on the menu.

Essential phrases:
- "Soy vegetariana" (I'm vegetarian)
- "Soy alérgica a los frutos secos" (I'm allergic to nuts)
- "¿Lleva carne este plato?" (Does this dish have meat?)
- "¿Tiene opciones sin gluten?" (Do you have gluten-free options?)
- "Sin lactosa, por favor" (Without lactose, please)

### Scenario 4: Requesting the Bill

Practice requesting and paying the bill. Verify charges, decide whether to split the bill, and handle payment.

Essential phrases:
- "La cuenta, por favor" (The bill, please)
- "¿Cuánto es en total?" (How much is it in total?)
- "¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?" (Can I pay by card?)
- "Junto" (Together - one bill)
- "Separado" (Separate - split bill)

## Practice Patterns

- Successfully order a complete meal with drinks and special requests
- Navigate the menu and ask questions about unfamiliar dishes
- Communicate allergies and find safe food options
- Order breakfast/coffee using correct Spanish terminology
- Request bill, verify charges, and complete payment

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- "El menú" vs "la carta" → Both mean menu, but "menú del día" is the set menu, "carta" is full menu
- "Para llevar" not "para ir" → Use "para llevar" for takeaway, not literal translation
- Asking for "la cuenta" too early → Wait until you've finished eating; servers won't rush you
- "Yo quiero" → Better to say "Para mí..." or "Me gustaría..." (more polite)
- Saying "estoy alérgico carne" → Need "a": "Soy alérgico a la carne"
- Expecting quick service → Spanish dining is leisurely; don't expect check immediately after eating

## Cultural Notes

- **Meal times are different**: Lunch (la comida) is 2-4pm, dinner (la cena) is 9-11pm. Many restaurants closed between 4-8pm.
- **Menú del día**: Affordable set menu at lunch (usually 10-15 euros). Includes starter, main, dessert, drink. Great value for travelers.
- **Bread and olives**: Often brought automatically to table. Usually included, but verify if charged.
- **Water not free**: Tap water safe but not commonly served. Order "agua" (bottled water). Specify "sin gas" (still) or "con gas" (sparkling).
- **Coffee culture**: "Café solo" is espresso. "Café con leche" is coffee with lots of milk (breakfast drink). "Cortado" is espresso with a little milk.
- **Tipping**: Not mandatory. Service usually included. Leaving 5-10% or rounding up is appreciated but not expected.
- **Getting attention**: Say "Disculpe" or "Por favor" to get server's attention. Waving or snapping is rude.
- **Leisurely dining**: Meals are social events. Tables are yours for the evening. You won't be rushed. Ask for "la cuenta" when ready.
- **Tapas culture**: In some regions, tapas come free with drinks. In others, you order them separately. Prices vary by region.
- **No substitutions**: Spanish kitchens less flexible than some countries. Special requests should be simple (sin cebolla, sin tomate).
