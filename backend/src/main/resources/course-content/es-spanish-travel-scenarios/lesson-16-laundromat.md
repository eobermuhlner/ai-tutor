## Lesson Goals

- Use self-service laundromats successfully
- Understand washing machine and dryer controls
- Request dry cleaning services

## Grammar Focus

### Giving Instructions for Laundry

**Rule:** Use infinitive verbs for instructions: "Lavar" (To wash), "Secar" (To dry).

Examples:
- "Quiero lavar esta ropa" (I want to wash these clothes)
- "Necesito secar" (I need to dry)

### Describing Clothing Care

**Rule:** Use "en seco" (dry), "a mano" (by hand), or temperature descriptions for care instructions.

Examples:
- "Lavar en seco" (Dry clean)
- "Agua fría" (Cold water)
- "No planchar" (Do not iron)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Using a Self-Service Laundromat

Practice using a coin-operated laundromat. Understand machine controls, get change, and complete laundry cycle.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Cómo funciona la lavadora?" (How does the washing machine work?)
- "¿Tiene cambio de cinco euros?" (Do you have change for five euros?)
- "¿Dónde se compra el detergente?" (Where do you buy detergent?)
- "¿Cuánto cuesta un ciclo?" (How much does one cycle cost?)
- "¿Cuánto tiempo tarda?" (How long does it take?)

### Scenario 2: Drop-Off Laundry Service

Practice using a wash-and-fold service. Describe what you need washed and when to pick up.

Essential phrases:
- "Quiero lavar esta ropa" (I want to wash these clothes)
- "¿Cuánto cobran por kilo?" (How much do you charge per kilo?)
- "¿Cuándo estará lista?" (When will it be ready?)
- "Mañana por la tarde" (Tomorrow afternoon)
- "¿A qué hora cierran?" (What time do you close?)

### Scenario 3: Dry Cleaning

Practice requesting dry cleaning services. Point out stains and special care instructions.

Essential phrases:
- "Necesito limpiar esta chaqueta en seco" (I need to dry clean this jacket)
- "Hay una mancha aquí" (There's a stain here)
- "¿Pueden quitarla?" (Can you remove it?)
- "Delicado, por favor" (Delicate, please)
- "¿Cuándo puedo recogerlo?" (When can I pick it up?)

### Scenario 4: Machine Malfunction

Practice handling when a laundry machine malfunctions. Seek help and explain the problem.

Essential phrases:
- "La lavadora no funciona" (The washing machine doesn't work)
- "Ha cogido mi dinero pero no ha empezado" (It took my money but didn't start)
- "Mi ropa está dentro" (My clothes are inside)
- "¿Puede ayudarme?" (Can you help me?)

## Practice Patterns

- Successfully complete self-service wash and dry cycle
- Drop off laundry and pick up at appointed time
- Request dry cleaning with special instructions
- Get change and buy detergent at laundromat
- Handle machine malfunction and seek assistance

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Not having exact change → Many machines coin-operated, need exact change
- "Rentar la lavadora" → "Usar la lavadora" (don't say "rentar" for using machines)
- Overloading machines → Follow capacity signs or clothes won't clean properly
- Using too much detergent → European machines need less detergent than you think
- Leaving laundry unattended → In busy laundromats, remove promptly or someone will remove it for you
- "¿Cuándo termina?" → "¿Cuánto tarda?" (asking about duration, not end time)

## Cultural Notes

- **Self-service laundromats less common**: Not as widespread as in US. Hotels often have laundry service. Ask hotel first.
- **Dry cleaners more common**: "Tintorerías" everywhere in cities. Often offer wash-and-fold service too, not just dry cleaning.
- **Coin-operated machines**: Laundromats use coins or tokens. Bring change or buy tokens from attendant/vending machine.
- **Detergent**: Some machines provide detergent automatically (built in). Others require you to buy from vending machine or bring your own.
- **Wash and fold service**: Many tintorerías offer "lavado y planchado" - they wash, dry, fold, and even iron your clothes. Charged by weight (3-5 euros/kg).
- **Quick turnaround**: Dry cleaners usually 2-3 days. Express service ("urgente") available for higher fee - same day or next day.
- **No 24-hour laundromats**: Unlike some countries, laundromats have set hours (typically 8am-9pm). Check hours.
- **Hand washing in hotels**: Many budget travelers hand wash in hotel sink/bathtub. Hotels generally allow this for underwear/socks.
- **Temperature in Celsius**: Washing machines show 30°, 40°, 60° etc. 30° = cold, 40° = warm, 60° = hot.
- **Smaller loads**: European washers smaller than American ones. Don't overload or clothes won't clean.
