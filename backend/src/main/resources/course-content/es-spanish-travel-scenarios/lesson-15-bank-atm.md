## Lesson Goals

- Successfully use Spanish ATMs
- Understand ATM screen options and vocabulary
- Exchange currency when needed
- Report lost or stolen cards
- Understand banking fees and exchange rates

## Grammar Focus

### Expressing Need for Money

**Rule:** Use "Necesito..." (I need...) to express financial needs: "Necesito sacar dinero" (I need to withdraw money).

Examples:
- "Necesito cambiar dinero" (I need to exchange money)
- "Necesito un cajero" (I need an ATM)

### Numbers for Money

**Rule:** Use numbers with "euros" for amounts: "Cien euros" (100 euros), "Veinte euros" (20 euros).

Examples:
- "Quiero sacar cien euros" (I want to withdraw 100 euros)
- "¿Cuál es el cambio?" (What's the exchange rate?)

## Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Using an ATM

Practice withdrawing cash from an ATM. Navigate Spanish menu screens and select appropriate options.

Essential phrases:
- "¿Dónde hay un cajero?" (Where is there an ATM?)
- "Necesito sacar dinero" (I need to withdraw money)
- Screen: "Seleccione idioma" → Choose "Inglés" or navigate in Spanish
- Screen: "Retirada" → Withdrawal option
- Screen: "¿Cantidad?" → Select amount (20, 50, 100, 200, or "Otra cantidad")

### Scenario 2: Exchanging Currency

Practice exchanging foreign currency at a bank or exchange office. Ask about rates and fees.

Essential phrases:
- "Quiero cambiar dólares a euros" (I want to exchange dollars to euros)
- "¿Cuál es el tipo de cambio?" (What's the exchange rate?)
- "¿Hay comisión?" (Is there a fee?)
- "Cien dólares" (100 dollars)

### Scenario 3: Reporting a Lost or Stolen Card

Practice reporting a banking emergency. Contact bank to block card and request help.

Essential phrases:
- "He perdido mi tarjeta" (I've lost my card)
- "Necesito bloquear mi tarjeta" (I need to block my card)
- "¿Puede ayudarme?" (Can you help me?)
- "El número de la tarjeta es..." (The card number is...)

### Scenario 4: ATM Card Retention Issue

Practice handling situation where ATM retains your card. Seek help from bank.

Essential phrases:
- "El cajero ha retenido mi tarjeta" (The ATM has retained my card)
- "Necesito recuperar mi tarjeta" (I need to recover my card)
- "¿Está el banco abierto?" (Is the bank open?)
- "Es urgente" (It's urgent)

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Not checking fees → Foreign ATMs often charge 3-5 euro fee + your bank's fee
- Using exchange bureaus → Banks usually have better rates than tourist exchange offices
- "Dinero" alone → Say "sacar dinero" or "retirar dinero" for withdrawal
- Panic when card retained → Stay calm, card usually retrievable during bank hours
- Taking large amounts → Daily limits exist (usually 200-600 euros per day)
- Not selecting "decline conversion" → If ATM offers to convert, decline! Your bank's rate better

## Cultural Notes

- **ATM fees**: Spanish ATMs may charge 2-5 euros per withdrawal for foreign cards. Your home bank may add additional fees. Withdraw larger amounts less frequently.
- **ATM locations**: Found everywhere - banks, shopping centers, train stations. Look for "Cajero Automático" sign.
- **Language options**: Most ATMs offer English. But good to learn Spanish options as backup. Not all ATMs have English.
- **Card retention**: If ATM malfunctions and keeps card, go to bank during business hours. They can retrieve it. Bring ID.
- **Currency conversion trick**: ATMs may offer to "convert at guaranteed rate." ALWAYS DECLINE. This "Dynamic Currency Conversion" gives terrible rate. Let your home bank convert instead.
- **Daily limits**: Withdrawal limits vary by ATM and your bank (typically 200-600 euros/day). Plan ahead for large purchases.
- **Bank hours**: Banks typically 8:30am-2pm weekdays only. ATMs work 24/7 but card retrieval needs bank hours.
- **Exchange offices**: "Casas de cambio" in tourist areas have convenient hours but worse rates and high fees. Use only if necessary.
- **Credit vs debit**: Debit cards usually have lower fees for ATM withdrawals than credit cards. Credit cards often treat withdrawals as "cash advance" with immediate interest.
- **Notify your bank**: Before traveling, tell your bank you'll be in Spain. Otherwise they may block card as fraud prevention.
