## Lesson Goals

- Read simple IPA transcriptions of familiar English words
- Recognize common vowel and consonant symbols
- Understand basic IPA notation conventions
- Practice pronouncing words from IPA transcriptions
- Navigate dictionary pronunciation guides

## Grammar Focus

### What is IPA and Why Use It?

**Rule:** IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) shows **how words actually sound**, not how they're spelled. One symbol = one sound.

Why IPA matters:
- English spelling is inconsistent: "cough" **[kɔf]**, "through" **[θɹu]**, "though" **[ðoʊ]** - all different!
- Dictionaries use IPA to show pronunciation
- Language learners use IPA to learn correct pronunciation
- IPA works for ALL languages (universal system)

**Notation:**
- **[square brackets]** = exact pronunciation: **[kæt]**
- Letters look similar to English alphabet, but represent specific sounds

### Essential English Vowels

**Rule:** English has more vowel sounds than the 5 letters A, E, I, O, U. IPA shows the actual sounds.

**Short vowels:**
- **[ɪ]** - as in "sit" **[sɪt]**, "big" **[bɪɡ]**
- **[ɛ]** - as in "bed" **[bɛd]**, "get" **[ɡɛt]**
- **[æ]** - as in "cat" **[kæt]**, "bad" **[bæd]**
- **[ʌ]** - as in "but" **[bʌt]**, "cup" **[kʌp]**
- **[ʊ]** - as in "book" **[bʊk]**, "good" **[ɡʊd]**

**Long vowels:**
- **[i]** or **[iː]** - as in "see" **[si]**, "feet" **[fit]**
- **[ɑ]** or **[ɑː]** - as in "father" **[ˈfɑðɚ]**, "car" **[kɑɹ]**
- **[ɔ]** or **[ɔː]** - as in "thought" **[θɔt]**, "law" **[lɔ]**
- **[u]** or **[uː]** - as in "boot" **[but]**, "food" **[fud]**

**The schwa [ə]** - most common English vowel (unstressed syllables):
- "about" **[əˈbaʊt]**
- "sofa" **[ˈsoʊfə]**
- "banana" **[bəˈnænə]**

Practice reading:
- "sit" **[sɪt]** vs. "seat" **[sit]** - different vowels!
- "cat" **[kæt]** vs. "cot" **[kɑt]** - **[æ]** vs. **[ɑ]**
- "book" **[bʊk]** vs. "boot" **[but]** - **[ʊ]** vs. **[u]**

### Essential English Consonants

**Rule:** Most English consonants have simple IPA symbols close to English letters.

**Simple consonants (same as spelling):**
- **[p]**, **[b]**, **[t]**, **[d]**, **[k]**, **[ɡ]** - "pat", "bat", "tap", "dad", "cat", "gap"
- **[m]**, **[n]** - "man", "no"
- **[f]**, **[v]**, **[s]**, **[z]** - "fan", "van", "sit", "zip"
- **[l]** - "love", "tell"
- **[h]** - "hat", "hello"

**Special symbols to learn:**
- **[ʃ]** - "sh" sound: "ship" **[ʃɪp]**, "fish" **[fɪʃ]**
- **[ʒ]** - "zh" sound: "measure" **[ˈmɛʒɚ]**, "vision" **[ˈvɪʒən]**
- **[tʃ]** - "ch" sound: "church" **[tʃɝtʃ]**, "watch" **[wɑtʃ]**
- **[dʒ]** - "j" sound: "judge" **[dʒʌdʒ]**, "age" **[eɪdʒ]**
- **[θ]** - "th" (voiceless): "think" **[θɪŋk]**, "bath" **[bæθ]**
- **[ð]** - "th" (voiced): "this" **[ðɪs]**, "bathe" **[beɪð]**
- **[ŋ]** - "ng" sound: "sing" **[sɪŋ]**, "song" **[sɔŋ]**
- **[ɹ]** - English "r": "red" **[ɹɛd]**, "car" **[kɑɹ]**
- **[j]** - "y" sound: "yes" **[jɛs]**, "you" **[ju]**
- **[w]** - "w" sound: "we" **[wi]**, "away" **[əˈweɪ]**

Practice reading:
- "ship" **[ʃɪp]** vs. "sip" **[sɪp]** - **[ʃ]** vs. **[s]**
- "thin" **[θɪn]** vs. "this" **[ðɪs]** - **[θ]** vs. **[ð]**
- "church" **[tʃɝtʃ]** vs. "judge" **[dʒʌdʒ]** - **[tʃ]** vs. **[dʒ]**

### Reading Simple Words

**Rule:** Put symbols together left to right to read whole words.

Examples:
- **[kæt]** = "cat"
- **[dɔɡ]** = "dog"
- **[haʊs]** = "house"
- **[bʊk]** = "book"
- **[fri]** = "free"

Multi-syllable words:
- **[ˈhæpi]** = "happy" (**[ˈ]** shows stress on first syllable)
- **[təˈdeɪ]** = "today" (stress on second syllable)
- **[ˈwɔtɚ]** = "water"
- **[kəmˈpjutɚ]** = "computer"

## Conversation Scenarios

### Reading Dictionary Entries

Practice reading pronunciation guides from English dictionaries. The tutor will present IPA transcriptions and ask you to pronounce them.

Key phrases:
- "This word is pronounced **[ˈhæpi]**"
- "The stress is on the first syllable"
- "I see the schwa **[ə]** in the second syllable"
- "The vowel is **[ɪ]**, not **[i]**"

### Pronouncing Familiar Words

Practice reading IPA transcriptions of common English words aloud.

Key phrases:
- "Let me sound out each symbol: **[k]** - **[æ]** - **[t]** = cat"
- "The **[ʃ]** symbol makes the 'sh' sound"
- "I need to distinguish **[ɪ]** and **[i]**"

### Comparing Similar Sounds

Practice hearing and producing differences between similar vowels and consonants.

Key phrases:
- "**[ɪ]** in 'sit' is different from **[i]** in 'seat'"
- "**[ʊ]** in 'book' vs. **[u]** in 'boot'"
- "**[θ]** is voiceless (think) and **[ð]** is voiced (this)"

## Practice Patterns

1. **Symbol recognition:** Match IPA symbols to their sounds: **[ʃ]** = "sh", **[θ]** = "th"
2. **Read simple words:** Practice reading **[kæt]**, **[dɔɡ]**, **[haʊs]**, **[bʊk]**
3. **Vowel discrimination:** Distinguish **[ɪ]/[i]**, **[ʊ]/[u]**, **[ɛ]/[æ]**
4. **Schwa recognition:** Find **[ə]** in multi-syllable words
5. **Stress placement:** Identify which syllable has **[ˈ]** (primary stress)
6. **Read sentences:** "**[aɪ ɡoʊ tu ðə stɔɹ]**" = "I go to the store"
7. **Minimal pairs:** Compare "ship" **[ʃɪp]** vs. "sip" **[sɪp]**

## Common Mistakes to Watch

- Confusing **[ɪ]** and **[i]** → "sit" is **[sɪt]** (short), "seat" is **[sit]** (long)
- Reading **[ʃ]** as "s-h" → **[ʃ]** is one sound "sh", not two letters
- Forgetting **[ə]** schwa → "about" is **[əˈbaʊt]**, not **[æˈbaʊt]**
- **[θ] vs. [ð]** → "think" has voiceless **[θ]**, "this" has voiced **[ð]**
- **[ʊ] vs. [u]** → "book" is **[bʊk]** (short), "boot" is **[but]** (long)
- Missing stress mark → Stress **[ˈ]** shows which syllable is emphasized
- **[ŋ]** as "n-g" → "sing" is **[sɪŋ]** (one sound), not **[s]**-**[ɪ]**-**[n]**-**[ɡ]**
- **[j]** as "j" → **[j]** is the "y" sound in "yes", not "j" in "judge"
- **[ɹ]** vs. "r" → English uses **[ɹ]** (approximant), not rolled "r"
- **[æ] vs. [ɛ]** → "cat" has **[æ]** (lower), "pet" has **[ɛ]** (higher)

## Cultural Notes

- American and British English use different vowels in many words: "hot" (US **[hɑt]**, UK **[hɒt]**)
- Dictionaries may use simplified IPA or their own pronunciation systems (not full IPA)
- The schwa **[ə]** is the most common vowel sound in English but has no dedicated spelling letter
- English spelling is irregular because it reflects historical pronunciation (Great Vowel Shift, 1400s-1600s)
- Native speakers often don't realize how many vowel sounds English has (often thinking there are only 5!)
- Regional accents in English can change IPA transcriptions significantly
- Learning IPA helps with spelling by showing the disconnect between letters and sounds
